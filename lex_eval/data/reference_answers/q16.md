## 1. Question

> With regards to Scotland, what mechanisms could the Scottish Government consider if it wanted to lessen the requirements for a single local authority to provide accommodation to the homeless, include any tangential or related measures which might free up local authority housing supply or budgets

Legislation and case law are both in scope.

**Scope of this answer:** Covers the levers available to the Scottish Government to reduce what a single local authority must do to accommodate homeless households, and the tangential measures that free up local authority housing supply or budgets. Limited to the statutory framework in Part II of the Housing (Scotland) Act 1987 and the orders made under it. Whether any of these changes would be desirable as policy, and their compatibility with the ECHR, are out of scope.

### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The Scottish Government has levers at three different heights, and the distinction between them is the practical point: **some need an Act of the Scottish Parliament, some can be done by order, and one significant lever is currently switched off by an order the Government itself made.** The single easiest mistake here is to assume that a duty written on the face of the Act can only be changed by an Act. This Act attaches its modification powers in their own sections, so each duty has to be checked against them.

- **Currently switched off.** The section 33 local connection referral power, which is the statutory route by which one authority moves the permanent accommodation duty to another, **cannot presently be used between Scottish authorities**. The Homeless Persons (Suspension of Referrals between Local Authorities) (Scotland) Order 2022 ([ssi/2022/356](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2022/356)) suspends it. Revoking or narrowing that Order is the single quickest way to relieve pressure on one authority, and it needs no primary legislation.
- **Ministerial, by order or regulations.** The standard of interim accommodation is set by the Homeless Persons (Unsuitable Accommodation) (Scotland) Order 2014 ([ssi/2014/243](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/243)), made under section 29(3). It has already been amended to create exemptions and can be amended again. Regulation-making powers in sections 31(3)(b), 32(3), 32(6) and 32B(5) and (6) allow prescribed matters to be varied, and section 32B(6) permits **different provision for different areas**, which is the express route to relieving a single authority rather than all 32.
- **Ministerial, and the most direct lever of all.** Section 32A provides that section 31(2), so far as it requires the accommodation to be permanent, **does not apply in such circumstances as may be prescribed** ([s.32A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/32A)). The permanent accommodation requirement is therefore not fixed by the Act in the way the duty itself is, and reducing it does **not** need an Act of the Scottish Parliament.
- **Check before assuming primary legislation is needed.** The duty in section 31(2) to secure that accommodation becomes available at all, the interim duty in section 29(1) and the definition of homelessness in section 24 have no ministerial modification power attached to them in the text retrieved for this answer. That is a reason to check each one, not a finding: sections 32A and 33A show that this Act attaches its modification powers in separate sections, easily missed by a search aimed at the duty itself.

**Tangential measures already in the Act** are often overlooked and free up stock or budget without changing any duty: section 35(1) lets an authority discharge the duty using **someone else's accommodation**, section 31(2A) to (2H) lets a **private accommodation offer** end the duty in a restricted case, section 35(2) allows **reasonable charges** to be recovered, section 38 compels **co-operation** from other authorities and registered social landlords, section 32(2) funds **prevention** by requiring reasonable steps before homelessness occurs, and section 32(4) preserves an authority's right to **recover vacant possession**.

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The duty that would be lessened

Two duties must be kept apart, because they have different levers.

**The interim duty**, section 29(1): where the authority has reason to believe an applicant may be homeless, it "shall secure that accommodation is made available for his occupation" pending inquiries, pending review, and until accommodation under section 31 becomes available. Section 29(2) provides that this duty "arises irrespective of any local connection which an applicant may have with the district of another local authority" ([ukpga/1987/26, s.29](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/29)). Section 29(3) lets the Scottish Ministers specify by order accommodation that does not count.

**The permanent accommodation duty**, section 31(2): where the authority is satisfied the applicant is homeless and not intentionally so, it "shall, **unless they notify another local authority in accordance with section 33** (referral of application on ground of local connection), secure that permanent accommodation becomes available for his occupation" ([s.31](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/31)).

The words in bold are where the single-authority burden is decided. Section 31(2) is drafted with the referral route as its only built-in exit.

#### 2.2 The lever that is currently disabled

> **Article 2 - Suspension of referrals**
> A relevant local authority **may not exercise the power under section 33(1) of the 1987 Act** (referral of application to another local authority) in respect of any other relevant local authority.

([ssi/2022/356, art.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2022/356/article/2))

"Relevant local authority" means a Scottish council ([art.1(2)](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2022/356/article/1)), so referrals to authorities in England and Wales, which section 33(1) also permits, are unaffected. Article 3 preserves the position for applications made before the Order came into force.

This matters more than any other single point in the answer. Section 33 sets out the conditions for referral, that neither the applicant nor anyone reasonably expected to reside with them has a local connection with the notified-to authority's district, that one of them does have such a connection with the other district, and that there is no risk of domestic abuse there ([s.33](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/33)); section 34 then moves the section 31(2) duty to the notified authority once the conditions are determined to be met ([s.34](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/34)). All of that machinery is presently unavailable between Scottish councils.

Any analysis that offers section 33 as the answer to "how could one authority's burden be reduced", without noting that it is suspended, is describing a mechanism that cannot be used today. Conversely, the suspension is itself among the cheapest levers, and the enabling power says so in terms.

> **Section 33A - Power to modify section 33**
> (1) The Scottish Ministers may by order made by statutory instrument provide that, for so long as the order is in force, the power of a local authority under subsection (1) of section 33 ... (a) **shall not be exercisable by such local authorities or such descriptions of local authority as are specified in the order, or shall be exercisable ... only in such circumstances, or with such modifications, as are specified in the order**; (b) [the same, in relation to the authorities referred to] ...
> (3) A statutory instrument containing an order under this section is, if made without a draft having been laid before, and approved by a resolution of, the Scottish Parliament, subject to annulment ...

([ukpga/1987/26, s.33A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/33A))

So the power is not simply on or off. Section 33A(1) expressly allows the suspension to be applied to **named authorities or descriptions of authority**, and to be made subject to **specified circumstances or modifications**. Narrowing the 2022 Order to relieve one authority, rather than revoking it nationally, is within the power as drafted. Section 33A(2) allows consequential modification of Part II and transitional provision, and section 33B requires Ministers to publish, and keep under review, a statement of the circumstances and criteria by reference to which the section 33A power is to be exercised ([s.33B](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/33B)). That statement is a procedural step to plan for, not a legal obstacle.

#### 2.3 The permanent accommodation requirement is itself ministerial

This is the provision most easily missed, and it changes the shape of the answer.

> **Section 32A - Power of the Scottish Ministers to modify application of sections 31 and 32**
> (1) The provisions of—
> (a) **section 31(2) so far as requiring that accommodation is to be permanent accommodation** (within the meaning of section 31(5)), and
> (b) section 32(5)(b),
> **do not apply in such circumstances as may be prescribed**.
> (2) Where— (a) accommodation has been provided under section 31(2), and (b) by virtue of subsection (1) above, that accommodation is not permanent accommodation ... or does not meet the special needs of the applicant and any other person referred to in section 24(2), section 26 does not apply.

([ukpga/1987/26, s.32A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/32A))

Three things follow.

- **The permanence requirement is severable from the duty.** Section 32A(1)(a) reaches section 31(2) "so far as requiring that accommodation is to be permanent accommodation", and nothing else in it. The duty to secure accommodation survives; what can be switched off by regulations is the requirement that what is secured be permanent.
- **It is done by prescribing circumstances.** The disapplication operates "in such circumstances as may be prescribed", so it is subordinate legislation and it can be targeted rather than general. Which instrument "prescribed" means here, and whether it can be targeted at a single named authority as sections 32(6) and 32B(6) expressly permit for their own regulations, is not stated in section 32A itself and was not traced through the Act's general provisions.
- **Section 32A(2) handles the consequence.** Where accommodation is non-permanent by virtue of subsection (1), section 26 (intentional homelessness) does not apply, so an applicant is not later treated as having made themselves homeless by moving on from it.

What section 32A does **not** do is define the circumstances. That is left entirely to regulations, and this research did not establish whether any have been made. Before relying on the section, check whether the power has been exercised and what is currently prescribed under it. **Section 31(5) sets the boundary the power operates against**: "permanent accommodation" includes accommodation secured by a Scottish secure tenancy, by a short Scottish secure tenancy where paragraph 1, 2 or 2A of schedule 6 to the Housing (Scotland) Act 2001 is satisfied, or by a private residential tenancy ([s.31](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/31)).

#### 2.4 Other ministerial levers inside the Act

**Interim accommodation standards, section 29(3).** The Ministers may specify by order accommodation that does not satisfy the interim duty. The order in force is the Unsuitable Accommodation Order 2014. Article 4 makes accommodation unsuitable "in all circumstances" if it is not wind and watertight, not suitable for occupation by a homeless household taking into account the needs of the household, or not meeting minimum safety standards ([ssi/2014/243, art.4](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/243/article/4)). Article 5 adds further disqualifying features, including accommodation outwith the authority's own area in which the household has not agreed to be placed ([art.5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/243/article/5)). Articles 6 and 7A carry exemptions, and article 7A is the proof of concept: it already disapplies parts of article 5 for community hosting, rapid access accommodation and shared tenancy accommodation ([art.7A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/243/article/7A)). Widening those exemptions enlarges the pool of lawful interim accommodation without touching the duty.

**Prescribed matters, sections 31(3)(b), 32(3), 32(6) and 32B.** Section 32(3) requires advice and assistance "of such type as may be prescribed" to applicants threatened with homelessness who fall outside the section 32(2) duty. Section 32(6) provides that regulations made by virtue of section 31(3)(b) or section 32(3) "may make different provision for different purposes and different areas" ([s.32](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/32)). Section 32B requires assessment for, and provision of, prescribed housing support services where a section 31(2) or 32(2) duty is owed, and section 32B(6) again allows "different provision for different purposes and different areas" ([s.32B](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/32B)).

The area-differentiation words are the direct answer to a question about **a single** local authority. They are what allows a targeted, rather than national, adjustment.

#### 2.5 Tangential measures that free up supply or budget

These change nothing about the duty and are available now.

- **Discharge using other people's accommodation, section 35(1).** An authority may perform a section 31 or 34 duty by making its own stock available, "by securing that he obtains accommodation from some other person", or by giving advice and assistance that secures accommodation from some other person ([s.35](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/35)). The duty is to secure accommodation, not to allocate council housing.
- **Private accommodation offers, section 31(2A) to (2H).** In a restricted case the duty ends if the applicant accepts or refuses a private accommodation offer: a short assured tenancy of at least 12 months, made with the authority's approval under arrangements with the landlord, where the authority is satisfied it is reasonable to accept ([s.31](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/31)).
- **Charging, section 35(2).** An authority may require reasonable charges for accommodation it secures, or reasonable amounts in respect of sums it pays another person for accommodation.
- **Compelled co-operation, section 38.** On request, another Scottish, English or Welsh authority, a development corporation, a registered housing association or a social work authority "shall co-operate in rendering such assistance ... as is reasonable in the circumstances" ([s.38](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1987/26/section/38)). This is a lever a single pressured authority can pull without any change in the law.
- **Prevention, section 32(2).** Where an applicant is threatened with homelessness and not intentionally so, the authority must "take reasonable steps to secure that accommodation does not cease to be available". Preventing a homelessness application is cheaper than discharging the duty that follows one.
- **Vacant possession preserved, section 32(4).** Nothing in the section 32(2) duty affects an authority's right to secure vacant possession, whether by contract or by enactment.

#### 2.6 The constraint the Supreme Court has settled

[Glasgow City Council v X (Scotland) [2025] UKSC 13](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2025/13) concerned the interim duty under section 29(1) read with article 4(b) of the 2014 Order, and specifically whether interim accommodation is unsuitable unless it meets **all** the needs of the applicant and every member of the household.

The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal, upholding the Inner House. The needs of the household must be **taken into account**, but they do not all have to be **met** at the interim stage: the household's need for a five-apartment property "had to be considered but did not have to be met at the interim stage" ([59]).

For this question the decision cuts in the Government's favour: the interim standard is less demanding than it might have been, and the room it leaves is created by the wording of a **ministerial order** that the Government can amend.

#### 2.7 What each option costs

| Lever | Instrument needed |
| --- | --- |
| Revoke, time-limit or narrow the suspension of section 33 referrals, including for named authorities only | Order under section 33A, with the section 33B statement kept up to date |
| Disapply the requirement that section 31(2) accommodation be permanent, in prescribed circumstances | Subordinate legislation prescribing those circumstances under section 32A |
| Widen the exemptions from the unsuitable accommodation standards | Order under section 29(3) |
| Vary prescribed advice, assistance or housing support, including for one area only | Regulations under ss.31(3)(b), 32(3), 32(6), 32B(5)-(6) |
| Remove or qualify the duty in section 31(2) to secure accommodation at all, the section 29(1) interim duty, or the section 24 definition of homelessness | An Act of the Scottish Parliament, unless a modification power attached to them elsewhere in the Act was missed here |

### 6.1 Research plan

1. **Map the duty being lessened**
   Retrieve the homelessness duties in Part II of the Housing (Scotland) Act 1987, distinguishing the interim duty from the permanent accommodation duty, and identify each point in the scheme where the duty can be discharged, deferred, transferred or ended.

2. **Find the ministerial powers over the duties themselves**
