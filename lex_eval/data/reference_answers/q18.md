### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The provision is **section 154 of the Transport Act 2000** ([ukpga/2000/38, s.154](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/154)).

It empowers the Secretary of State, with the approval of the Treasury as respects England, to make grants to operators of eligible bus services towards their costs in operating those services. Critically, subsection (3) provides that such grants are "of such amount and **subject to such conditions** ... as may be determined by" the Secretary of State. The fare cap is **a condition of that grant funding**, so there is no statutory instrument establishing it.

The Act does contain **express maximum-fares powers**, and they should be distinguished rather than overlooked. Section 113E(5)(a) lets an advanced quality partnership scheme specify "requirements as to the maximum fares that may be charged" ([ukpga/2000/38, s.113E](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/113E)), and a local service contract under a franchising scheme fixes terms "including in particular as to frequency, **fares** and standard of service" ([ukpga/2000/38, s.123A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/123A)). Both belong to **local transport authorities** over their own areas, not to the Secretary of State, so neither can deliver a national cap. That is why a nationwide scheme runs on grant conditions instead.

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 Section 154, the operative provision

> **Section 154 — Grants to bus service operators**
>
> (1) The Secretary of State with the approval of the Treasury (as respects England) or the National Assembly for Wales (as respects Wales) may make grants to operators of eligible bus services towards their costs in operating those services.
> (2) The Secretary of State ... may make provision by regulations as to the method of calculation of grants.
> (3) Subject to the provisions of any such regulations, grants under this section shall be of such amount and **subject to such conditions** (including conditions requiring their repayment in specified circumstances) as may be determined by —
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the Secretary of State with the approval of the Treasury (as respects England), or
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) the National Assembly for Wales (as respects Wales).
> (4) A determination under subsection (3) may be made either generally or in relation to particular cases or classes of case.
> (5) In this section "eligible bus services" means bus services of a class (or using vehicles of a class) prescribed by regulations made by the Secretary of State (as respects England) or the National Assembly for Wales (as respects Wales).

([ukpga/2000/38, s.154](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/154))

Four features of the section carry the scheme:

- **The condition power is unenumerated (subsection (3)).** Nothing narrows what a condition of grant may require, so a maximum single fare is within it.
- **A determination can be made generally (subsection (4)).** This is what makes a scheme national in effect rather than a series of individual funding decisions.
- **Enforcement is by repayment (subsection (3)).** Conditions "requiring their repayment in specified circumstances" are expressly contemplated, so an operator that breaches the cap can be made to repay. No separate enforcement provision is needed.
- **Treasury approval is a precondition as respects England (subsections (1) and (3)(a)).** It is part of the power, not an administrative formality.

The boundary of the power is subsection (5): it operates on "eligible bus services", a class prescribed by regulations, so the cap reaches only services within that prescribed class.

#### 2.2 The express maximum-fares powers, and why they are not national

**Advanced quality partnership schemes.** Section 113E(5) provides:

> (5) The standard of services which may be specified in a scheme may also include —
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **requirements as to the maximum fares that may be charged** for particular journeys, or for journeys of particular descriptions, on services to which the scheme applies,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) requirements as to the ways in which passengers may pay for journeys, ...

([ukpga/2000/38, s.113E](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/113E))

Three limits keep this out of the answer to the question as asked:

- **The holder is a local transport authority.** Section 113C(1) provides that "a local transport authority whose area is in England, or two or more such authorities acting jointly, may make an advanced quality partnership scheme" ([ukpga/2000/38, s.113C](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/113C)).
- **Operators hold a veto.** Section 113E(7): a scheme "may include a requirement falling within subsection (4)(b) or (5)(a) only if there are **no admissible objections** to the requirement from relevant operators."
- **The reach is the scheme's services**, not the country.

The Secretary of State's role here is confined to making regulations about the content or operation of schemes containing such a requirement (s.113N(1)(b)(i)), not to setting fares.

**Franchising schemes.** Section 123A(5)(b) defines a local service contract as one under which the operator "undertakes to provide the local services on such terms (including in particular as to frequency, **fares** and standard of service) as may be specified". Again the power is the franchising authority's, and section 123A(4) defines that as "a local transport authority whose area is in England".

#### 2.3 Why the national cap is a grant condition and not an instrument

The searches recorded in `searches.json` covered the Transport Act 2000, the Transport Act 1985 and the Concessionary Bus Travel Act 2007, and returned no provision empowering **the Secretary of State** to set, cap or otherwise fix the fare a bus passenger is charged nationwide. Central government's powers over fares are exercised by **funding** (section 154, and section 154A below) or by **conferring a concession on a class of person** (2.4 below); the direct fare-fixing powers in 2.2 sit with local transport authorities.

That is the structural reason the answer to this question is a section of an Act plus a funding condition, rather than a named statutory instrument. A reader looking for an SI called something like "The Bus Fare Cap Regulations" will not find one, and its absence is not evidence that the citation is wrong.

#### 2.4 The concessionary regime is a different mechanism

The Concessionary Bus Travel Act 2007 ([ukpga/2007/13](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2007/13)) creates "the national concession" (s.1), with reimbursement of operators (s.3) and a power to vary its scope (s.8). Section 145A of the Transport Act 2000 ([ukpga/2000/38, s.145A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/145A)) provides for mandatory concessions in England for journeys not beginning on the London bus network.

A concession is an **entitlement held by a class of eligible person** (in the national concession's case, older and disabled passengers), reimbursed to the operator. A fare cap changes the maximum price payable by **any** passenger on a participating service. The two regimes are funded and enforced differently and should not be conflated.

#### 2.5 The parallel local grant power

**Section 154A of the Transport Act 2000** ([ukpga/2000/38, s.154A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/154A)) gives a local transport authority whose area is in England power to make grants to operators of local services with one or more stopping places in its area. Subsection (3) mirrors section 154(3): grants are "of such amount and subject to such conditions (including conditions requiring their repayment in specified circumstances) as may be determined by the authority".

Where the authority is a mayoral combined authority or mayoral CCA, subsections (4) and (5) make the function exercisable only by the mayor on the authority's behalf. Subsections (6) to (8) let the Secretary of State issue guidance, to which authorities and mayors must have regard.

Section 154A matters for this question because it means a capped fare in a particular area may rest on the **local** authority's power rather than on section 154. A national cap funded by central government rests on section 154.

#### 2.6 The parallel with Scotland

Section 154 is drawn in near-identical terms to **section 38 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001** ([asp/2001/2, s.38](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/38)), down to both provisions repealing section 92 of the Finance Act 1965 and section 111 of the Transport Act 1985. The equivalent Scottish question is therefore answered by the same statutory route under a different Act, with section 70 of the 2001 Act available as a wider alternative that section 154 does not have a direct counterpart to.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Transport Act 2000 is stated as extending to the United Kingdom, but section 154 is drawn on its face as an England and Wales provision, allocating the power between the Secretary of State (as respects England) and the National Assembly for Wales (as respects Wales). The Concessionary Bus Travel Act 2007 extends to England and Wales. This answer addresses England only.
- **Status of the provisions cited.** Sections 154, 154A and 145A of the Transport Act 2000 were returned as current revised text, with no indication of repeal. Section 154A and the mayoral CCA references in it are later insertions.
- **Limit of this answer.** That the Secretary of State holds no power to fix bus fares nationally is a negative established by not finding one across the three Acts searched. It is weaker evidence than a positive citation, and it is the proposition most worth a reviewer's attention. The express but local powers at sections 113E(5)(a) and 123A(5)(b) are by contrast positively established.
- **Out of scope.** The amount of funding allocated, the operators participating, the dates the £2 and then £3 caps ran, and the departmental announcements establishing them are administrative rather than legislative and cannot be confirmed from the legislation database.
