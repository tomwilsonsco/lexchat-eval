# What makes up the Scottish Administration, as defined by section 126

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

Section 126(6) of the Scotland Act 1998 defines the Scottish Administration as **the office-holders in the Scottish Administration and the members of the staff of the Scottish Administration**.

The office-holders divide in two under section 126(7)(a): **ministerial** (members of the Scottish Government and junior Scottish Ministers) and **non-ministerial**.

The non-ministerial offices are where the difficulty lies. Section 126(8) names **three** expressly, then adds "any other office of a description specified in an **Order in Council**".

**A complete list therefore cannot be produced from primary legislation alone.** It requires collecting every Order in Council made under section 126(8)(b), and an answer that offers a list without having done so should say the list is incomplete rather than present it as exhaustive.

---

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 The definition

> **Section 126 - Interpretation**
> (6) References in this Act and in any other enactment to the Scottish Administration are to **the office-holders in the Scottish Administration and the members of the staff of the Scottish Administration**.
> (7) For the purposes of this Act -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) references to office-holders in the Scottish Administration are to -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(i) members of the Scottish Government and junior Scottish Ministers, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) the holders of offices in the Scottish Administration which are not ministerial offices, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) references to members of the staff of the Scottish Administration are to the staff of the persons referred to in paragraph (a).
> (8) For the purposes of this Act, the offices in the Scottish Administration which are not ministerial offices are -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the Registrar General of Births, Deaths and Marriages for Scotland, the Keeper of the Registers of Scotland and the Keeper of the Records of Scotland, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **any other office of a description specified in an Order in Council** ...

([ukpga/1998/46, s.126](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/126))

Note that the Scottish Administration is defined as a body of **persons**, office-holders and staff, not as a set of organisations. A question phrased in terms of "organisations, offices etc." maps onto the office-holders limb.

Related provisions: section 44 constitutes the Scottish Government ([s.44](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/44)), section 48 deals with the Scottish Law Officers ([s.48](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/48)), and section 51 with the Civil Service ([s.51](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/51)).

### 2.2 The three offices named in the Act

Section 126(8)(a) names, exhaustively for that paragraph:

1. the Registrar General of Births, Deaths and Marriages for Scotland
2. the Keeper of the Registers of Scotland
3. the Keeper of the Records of Scotland

### 2.3 The open-ended limb, and two worked examples

Section 126(8)(b) is the reason the list cannot be closed from the Act. Offices are added by Order in Council, each specifying further offices. Two retrieved examples show the mechanism:

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2012** ([uksi/2012/3073, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2012/3073/article/2)):

> The following offices are specified for the purposes of section 126(8)(b) of the Scotland Act 1998 ... -
> (a) Her Majesty's inspector of constabulary appointed under section 71(2) of the Police and Fire Reform (Scotland) Act 2012;
> (b) Her Majesty's Chief Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...;
> (c) Her Majesty's Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...; and
> (d) Assistant Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2015** ([uksi/2015/200, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2015/200/article/2)):

> ... (a) the Clerk of the Sheriff Appeal Court appointed under section 59(1) of the Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014;
> (b) a Deputy Clerk of the Sheriff Appeal Court appointed under section 60(1) of that Act.

The 2012 Order also contains its own revocation provisions (articles 3 and 4), which is a reminder that the set of specified offices changes over time: later Orders both add and remove.

### 2.4 Why a complete list cannot be given here

Three features combine:

- The Act names three offices and then delegates the rest (s.126(8)(b)).
- The delegated instruments are numerous, spread over the years since 1999, and amend and revoke one another.
- Only two such Orders were retrieved in this research.

The honest answer is therefore **structural**: the Scottish Administration comprises the ministerial office-holders, the three offices in section 126(8)(a), the offices specified from time to time by Order in Council under section 126(8)(b), and the staff of all of them. A named list of the section 126(8)(b) offices requires a complete survey of those Orders as they now stand, which this research has not done.

Presenting the two Orders above as if they were the whole set would be wrong. They are examples of the mechanism, not an enumeration.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Scotland Act 1998 extends to the United Kingdom and was returned in revised form.
- **Limit of this answer.** The list of section 126(8)(b) offices is **incomplete by acknowledgement**, not by oversight. Two Orders were retrieved out of an unknown larger number, and no attempt is made here to state the current consolidated position.
- **Why that is the right answer.** Where the enabling provision delegates open-endedly and the instruments cannot all be retrieved, stating the framework and disclosing the gap is more useful, and more accurate, than a partial list presented as complete.
