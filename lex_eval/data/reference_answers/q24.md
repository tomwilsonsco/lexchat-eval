## 1. Question

> Is the Scotland Act 1998 in force?

Legislation and case law are both in scope.

**Scope of this answer:** Covers whether the Scotland Act 1998 is in force, and how that is established: the Act's own commencement provision and the commencement order made under it. The current content of the Act as amended, and the many later Acts that have amended it, are out of scope.

## 2. Reference answer

<!-- BEGIN REFERENCE ANSWER -->

### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**Yes. Every provision of the Scotland Act 1998 requiring commencement had been commenced by 1 April 2000, and the Act remains in force as amended.**

The route to that answer is the Act's own commencement provision read with the commencement order made under it:

- **Section 130(1)** put only part of the Act on an appointed-day footing: sections 19 to 43, Parts II to V, sections 117 to 124 and part of section 125. Everything else came into force on Royal Assent, 19 November 1998.
- **The Scotland Act 1998 (Commencement) Order 1998 ([uksi/1998/3178](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1998/3178))** then commenced the rest. Article 2(1) brought all remaining provisions into force on **1 July 1999**, except those in Schedule 5, and article 3 names 1 July 1999 as **the principal appointed day**. The Schedule 5 provisions followed on **1 April 2000**.

**In-force status must rest on legislation.** It is established by a commencement provision, a commencement instrument, or the absence of any commencement requirement, and by checking that the Act has not been repealed. A judgment that happens to apply an Act is corroboration at best. Most statutes have never been litigated, so the absence of case law proves nothing, and its presence proves only that the Act was in force at the time of the facts in that case, in the respects the court considered.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 Section 130: what needed commencing and what did not

> **Section 130 - Commencement**
> (1) Sections 19 to 43, Parts II to V, sections 117 to 124 and section 125 (except so far as relating to paragraphs 10, 11, 19 and 23(1) and (6) of Schedule 8) shall come into force on such day as the Secretary of State may by order appoint.
> (2) Different days may be appointed under this section for different purposes.

([ukpga/1998/46, s.130](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46/section/130))

This is a **partial** commencement provision, and reading it correctly matters. It does not say that the Act comes into force on an appointed day. It lists the provisions that do. Anything outside that list was in force on Royal Assent, without any order being needed, including the core provisions establishing the Scottish Parliament in Part I.

Section 132 confirms the citation, "This Act may be cited as the Scotland Act 1998" ([s.132](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46/section/132)), and section 131 records that section 25 extends only to Scotland ([s.131](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46/section/131)); the Act otherwise extends across the United Kingdom.

#### 2.2 The commencement order

The power in section 130(1) was exercised by the Scotland Act 1998 (Commencement) Order 1998:

> **Article 2 - Appointed days**
> (1) All the provisions of the Act, so far as not then in force, shall come into force on 1st July 1999, except the provisions specified in Schedule 5 to this Order.
> (2) The provisions of the Act, specified in column 1 of any of the Schedules to this Order ... shall, so far as not then in force, come into force on the day appointed in relation to that Schedule in the Table below ...

([uksi/1998/3178, art.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1998/3178/article/2))

The Table sets the appointed days as 25 January 1999 (Schedule 1), 1 April 1999 (Schedule 2), 6 May 1999 (Schedule 3), 20 May 1999 (Schedule 4) and 1 April 2000 (Schedule 5). Article 3 provides that "**The principal appointed day is 1st July 1999**" ([art.3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1998/3178/article/3)).

The staging follows the devolution timetable. Schedule 3 (6 May 1999) commenced sections 19 to 22 on the Presiding Officer, administration and proceedings, for the first election. Schedule 4 (20 May 1999) commenced section 27 on the participation of the Scottish Law Officers. Schedule 5, the last tranche, commenced section 120 on accounts and audit and part of section 125 on 1 April 2000.

The combined effect of article 2(1) and Schedule 5 is that **no provision of the Act remained uncommenced after 1 April 2000**.

#### 2.3 Why the case law route is the wrong route

A search of Find Case Law for Supreme Court judgments on the Scotland Act returns a substantial body of decisions applying it. In [For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2025/16), for instance, the Court set out how "Section 29 of the Scotland Act provides that a provision of an Act of the Scottish Parliament is outside the legislative competence of the Scottish Parliament if it relates to reserved matters", and how Schedule 5 specifies those matters, including "Equal opportunities" (section L2). That is a court treating sections 29 and Schedule 5 as operative in 2025. It is consistent with the Act being in force, and nothing more.

The reasoning does not run the other way, for three reasons:

1. **Absence proves nothing.** The great majority of statutes have never been the subject of a reported judgment. If a judgment were needed to show an Act was in force, most of the statute book could not be shown to be in force.
2. **A judgment is evidence of the past, and only of what it considered.** *For Women Scotland* shows the court treating section 29 and Schedule 5 as operative. It says nothing about section 130, section 120, or any other provision the court had no reason to look at, and nothing about a provision repealed since.
3. **It answers a different question.** Courts decide the disputes in front of them. Commencement is decided by Parliament in the Act, and by the Minister in the commencement instrument.

A statement that the Act is in force, supported only by a judgment that cites it, can be a correct conclusion reached by an unsound route. The correct answer, on the correct route, is section 130 plus the 1998 Order.

#### 2.4 A note on searching for the Act

Searching the legislation service for the phrase "Scotland Act 1998" returns instruments **made under** the Act, including the Commencement Order, but not the Act itself at [ukpga/1998/46](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46). Its provisions retrieve normally once requested by identifier. An answer that concludes the Act cannot be found, or that turns to case law because a title search came back without it, has drawn the wrong conclusion from a search limitation.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Scotland Act 1998 is an Act of the United Kingdom Parliament. It extends to the whole United Kingdom, except section 25 which extends only to Scotland (s.131).
- **Status.** In force as amended. Royal Assent 19 November 1998; every provision needing an appointed day had been commenced by 1 April 2000.
- **Amendment.** The Act has been amended many times since, notably by the Scotland Acts 2012 and 2016. "In force" is a statement about commencement and repeal, not about the Act remaining in its original terms.
- **Limit of this answer.** Establishes commencement. It does not verify provision by provision that nothing has since been repealed, which for a specific provision should be checked on the revised text.
