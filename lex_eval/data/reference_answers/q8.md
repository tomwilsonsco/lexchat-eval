### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**There is no general statutory definition of "shop" in Scots law.** Each Act that needs the word defines it for its own purposes, and the definitions differ from each other in ways that matter. An answer has to name the definitions it found and say which Act each belongs to.

The definitions below are the ones these searches reached. They are not put forward as the whole population, and a longer list found by a wider search is a better answer, not a contradictory one.

The second trap is currency. The **Shops Act 1950** is the statute most readily associated with this question, and **sections 38 and 39 of it are repealed**. Citing them as current law, or asserting that every provision relied on is in force, is the specific failure this question exists to catch.

| Act | Provision | Definition given |
|---|---|---|
| Christmas Day and New Year's Day Trading (Scotland) Act 2007 | s.7 | "shop" means any premises **within a building** where there is carried on a trade or business consisting **wholly or mainly of the retail sale of goods** |
| Employment Rights Act 1996 | s.232(3) | "shop" **includes any premises where any retail trade or business is carried on** |
| Employment Rights Act 1996 | s.43ZB(2)(a) | for "large shop" in that Part, "shop" means any premises where there is carried on a trade or business consisting wholly or mainly of the **sale of goods** |

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The Scottish definition: the 2007 Act

The one clean, Scotland-only definition retrieved is in the interpretation section of the Christmas Day and New Year's Day Trading (Scotland) Act 2007:

> **Section 7 - Interpretation**
> In this Act -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"large shop" means a shop which has a **relevant floor area exceeding 280 square metres**;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"relevant floor area" means so much of the floor area of a shop as is used for making retail sales or for the display of goods in connection with such sales;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"retail sale" means any sale of goods for consumption or use, whether or not by the buyer, for purposes unconnected with a trade or business;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"shop" means any premises within a building where there is carried on a trade or business consisting wholly or mainly of the retail sale of goods**

([asp/2007/13, s.7](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/13/section/7))

Two limits are built in and are easy to miss. The premises must be **within a building**, which excludes an open-air stall or a market pitch. And the trade must be **wholly or mainly** retail sale of goods, so a business selling mainly services is not a shop for this Act even if it also sells goods.

The definition does work only inside that Act: it drives the section 1 prohibition on large shops opening on Christmas Day ([s.1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/13/section/1)), the section 3 exemptions ([s.3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/13/section/3)) and the section 4 offence ([s.4](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2007/13/section/4)).

#### 2.2 The employment definition, which applies in Scotland

The Employment Rights Act 1996 extends across Great Britain and carries a **different and wider** definition, for the Sunday working rights of shop workers:

> **Section 232 - Shop workers**
> (1) In this Act "shop worker" means an employee who, under his contract of employment, is or may be required to do shop work.
> (2) In this Act "shop work" means work in or about a shop ... on a day on which the shop is open for the serving of customers.
> (3) Subject to subsection (4), in this Act **"shop" includes any premises where any retail trade or business is carried on**.
> (4) Where premises are used mainly for purposes other than those of retail trade or business and would not (apart from subsection (3)) be regarded as a shop, only such part of the premises as - (a) is used wholly or mainly for the purposes of retail trade or business, or (b) is used both ...

([ukpga/1996/18, s.232](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/18/section/232))

The contrast with the 2007 Act is the point of the question. Section 232(3) says "**includes any**" premises where "**any**" retail trade or business is carried on, with no building requirement and no "wholly or mainly" threshold at the level of the premises. Subsection (4) then handles mixed-use premises by treating **part** of them as the shop, which is a device the 2007 Act does not use at all.

The same Act carries a **third**, narrower definition for a different Part:

> **Section 43ZB - Interpretation**
> (1) In this Part - "large shop" means a shop which has a relevant floor area exceeding 280 square metres ...
> (2) In the definition of "large shop" in subsection (1) - (a) **"shop" means any premises where there is carried on a trade or business consisting wholly or mainly of the sale of goods**; (b) "relevant floor area" means the internal floor area of so much of the large shop in question as consists of or is comprised in a building.

([ukpga/1996/18, s.43ZB](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/18/section/43ZB))

Note this one says "**sale of goods**", not "retail sale of goods". One Act therefore holds two different definitions of the same word, which is the clearest possible demonstration that no single answer exists.

#### 2.3 Near misses that are not definitions of "shop"

Three retrieved sources look relevant and are not:

- **Protection of Workers (Retail and Age-restricted Goods and Services) (Scotland) Act 2021, s.5** defines "**retail premises**", not "shop": "premises that are used wholly or mainly for the sale or supply of goods, on a retail basis, to members of the public" ([asp/2021/6, s.5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2021/6/section/5)). A modern Scottish statute on retail workers deliberately avoids the word.
- **Licensing (Scotland) Act 2005** contains **no** definition of "shop". Its interpretation section defines "premises" as "any place and includes a vehicle, vessel or moveable structure" ([asp/2005/16, s.147](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2005/16/section/147)). Offering a "shop" definition from this Act is an error.
- **Tenancy of Shops (Scotland) Act 1964** has "shops" in its title but defines nothing. The sections retrieved are citation and extent ([s.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1964/50/section/2)) and the continuance of the 1949 Act ([s.1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1964/50/section/1)). The operative definition sits in the Tenancy of Shops (Scotland) Act 1949, which the search returned only as an untitled stub.

#### 2.4 The Shops Act 1950 and currency

Four searches for the Shops Act 1950 returned no such Act, only unrelated local legislation (Gateshead Extension Act 1953, Crosby Corporation Act 1956 and similar). The Act is not available to be read, so **its definitions cannot be quoted or relied on from these sources**, and sections 38 and 39 of it are in any event repealed.

The retrieval also shows that repeal is visible when an Act is held: the same searches returned "**Shops (Early Closing Days) Act 1965 (repealed 1.12.1994)**", with the repeal marked in the title itself. An answer that cannot see a repeal flag has not read the Act, and must not assert that the provisions it relies on are in force.

Relatedly, the **Sunday Trading Act 1994** was returned with **extent "England, Wales"** only. Sunday and holiday trading in Scotland is governed by the 2007 Act at 2.1 above, not by the 1994 Act and not by the Shops Act 1950.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The 2007 Act and the 2021 Act extend to Scotland. The Employment Rights Act 1996 was returned with United Kingdom extent and its shop worker provisions apply in Scotland. The Sunday Trading Act 1994 extends to England and Wales only.
- **Not exhaustive.** These are the definitions found by the searches run. Others certainly exist in Scots law, in planning, rating, licensing and health and safety legislation among others. A response that lists more of them is going further than this answer, not disagreeing with it.
- **Out of scope.** No definition is offered from the Shops Act 1950: it could not be retrieved, and the provisions usually cited from it are repealed.
- **Not addressed.** Case law on the meaning of "shop" is outside this question's legislation-only scope.
