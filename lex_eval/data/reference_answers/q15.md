## 2. Answer

# How "months" are defined under the Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**"Month" means calendar month**, and the definition comes from the **Scotland Act 1998 interpretation order of 1999**, not from the Interpretation and Legislative Reform (Scotland) Act 2010.

The 2010 Act does **not** apply to the Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009. Section 1(1)(a) of the 2010 Act applies Part 1 only to Acts of the Scottish Parliament "the Bills for which receive Royal Assent **on or after** the day on which this Part comes into force". Part 1 came into force on 6 April 2011; the 2009 Act preceded it.

The applicable definition is in Schedule 2 to the 1999 Order: **"'Month' means calendar month"**.

That a provision such as section 35ZA was inserted into the 2009 Act by a later Act makes no difference. The 2010 Act keys its application to the Royal Assent date of the **Act being interpreted**, not to when an individual provision was inserted into it.

---

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 The 2009 Act does not define the term

Section 98 of the Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009 is the Act's interpretation section ([asp/2009/12, s.98](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2009/12/section/98)). It defines terms specific to the Act's subject matter, "additional greenhouse gas", "baseline", "baseline year" and so on. It does not define "month". The meaning therefore comes from the general interpretation regime applicable to the Act.

### 2.2 Which regime applies, and why it is not the 2010 Act

> **Section 1 - Application of Part 1**
> (1) This Part applies to -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Acts of the Scottish Parliament the Bills for which receive Royal Assent on or after the day on which this Part comes into force**,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Scottish instruments made on or after that day ...
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) this Act.

([asp/2010/10, s.1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2010/10/section/1))

This is a **commencement-keyed** application provision, and it is the reason the 2010 Act is the wrong instrument here. The Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009 received Royal Assent in 2009. Part 1 of the 2010 Act came into force on 6 April 2011, the date used throughout the associated savings instrument ([ssi/2011/88](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2011/88)). The 2009 Act therefore falls outside section 1(1)(a).

Schedule 1 to the 2010 Act, which carries its definitions of words and expressions ([asp/2010/10, sch 1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2010/10/schedule/1)), is introduced by section 25(1) and sits inside Part 1. It is caught by the same limit.

### 2.3 The regime that does apply, and the definition

For Acts of the Scottish Parliament passed before 6 April 2011, interpretation is governed by the Scotland Act 1998 (Transitory and Transitional Provisions) (Publication and Interpretation etc. of Acts of the Scottish Parliament) Order 1999 ([uksi/1999/1379](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1999/1379)). Article 6 deals with statutory interpretation, Schedule 1 with statutory interpretation and operation, and Schedule 2 carries the general definitions.

Schedule 2 provides:

> **"Month" means calendar month;**

([uksi/1999/1379, sch 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1999/1379/schedule/2))

A calendar month runs from a day in one month to the corresponding day in the next, rather than as a fixed number of days.

### 2.4 Later insertion does not switch the regime

Section 35ZA was inserted into the 2009 Act by later legislation. That does not bring the 2010 Act into play, for a reason visible on the face of section 1(1)(a): the trigger is the Royal Assent date of the **Bill for the Act**, a single fixed date for the whole Act. It is not a provision-by-provision test, and an amending Act does not give the amended Act a new Royal Assent date.

The practical consequence is that one interpretation regime governs the whole of the 2009 Act, including provisions inserted into it years later. An answer that applies the 2010 Act to section 35ZA because section 35ZA is recent has misread the application provision.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009 and the Interpretation and Legislative Reform (Scotland) Act 2010 extend to Scotland. The 1999 Order is a UK statutory instrument made under the Scotland Act 1998.
- **Limit of this answer.** The 6 April 2011 commencement date for Part 1 of the 2010 Act is taken from the associated savings and transitional instrument rather than from a commencement order retrieved in terms. The reasoning does not depend on the precise date, only on its being later than the 2009 Act's Royal Assent.
