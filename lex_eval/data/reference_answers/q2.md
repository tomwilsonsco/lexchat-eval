# Powers of direction of the Scottish Ministers over Health Boards

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The Scottish Ministers hold a **broad general power of direction** over Health Boards under **section 2(5) of the National Health Service (Scotland) Act 1978**: in carrying out the purposes for which they were constituted, and in exercising any function otherwise conferred on them by or under that Act or the 2021 Act, each Health Board "shall act subject to, and in accordance with, such regulations as may be made, and such directions as may be given" by the Scottish Ministers — and those directions "may be made or given generally or to meet the circumstances of a particular area or matter". That general power is supplemented by two targeted powers engaged by failure: **section 78A** (direct that specified functions be performed by another body or person where a service is failing) and **section 77** (declare a body in default after an inquiry, whereupon its members vacate office). **Section 105(6)–(7)** supplies the general machinery: any direction may be varied or revoked by subsequent directions made in like manner, and may be given either generally or in specified cases subject to exceptions and conditions.

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 The constitutional starting point — Health Boards exercise the Ministers' own functions

Health Boards are not autonomous statutory bodies with functions of their own. Under **s.2(1)(a)**, the Scottish Ministers "shall by order constitute … boards for such areas as he may by order determine, **for the purpose of exercising such of his functions relating to the health service as he may so determine**", and for making arrangements on his behalf for the provision of the services in Part II. Special Health Boards are constituted on the same footing under **s.2(1)(b)**, again "for the purpose of exercising such of his functions … as he may so determine" ([NHS (Scotland) Act 1978, s.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/2)).

This is the doctrinal basis for the breadth of the direction power that follows: a Health Board is exercising delegated ministerial functions, and s.2(8) confirms that it does so "notwithstanding that it is exercising functions on behalf of the Secretary of State" (while remaining liable in its own name).

### 2.2 The general power of direction — s.2(5)

> **s.2(5):** "In carrying out the purposes mentioned in subsection (1) and in exercising any function otherwise conferred on them by or under this Act or by or under the 2021 Act each Health Board shall act subject to, and in accordance with, such regulations as may be made, and such directions as may be given, by the Secretary of State; and such regulations and directions may be made or given generally or to meet the circumstances of a particular area or matter."
> ([s.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/2))

Four features of the retrieved text are worth drawing out:

1. **Its reach is not confined to the delegated functions.** It bites both on "the purposes mentioned in subsection (1)" *and* on "any function **otherwise** conferred on them by or under this Act or by or under the 2021 Act". A function given to a Health Board directly by statute is therefore still exercisable only subject to ministerial direction.
2. **It is mandatory in form.** The Board "shall act subject to, and in accordance with" the directions. The text creates a duty of compliance rather than a duty to have regard — contrast the guidance provisions at 2.5 below.
3. **It is expressly both general and particular.** Directions "may be made or given generally or to meet the circumstances of a particular area or matter" — so a direction may be issued to all Boards at large or to one Board about one matter.
4. **No procedural conditions appear on the face of the subsection.** The retrieved text imposes no consultation, writing, or publication requirement on a s.2(5) direction. (See the caveat at 2.6.)

**Directions as to schemes of functions (s.2(6)–(7)).** Regulations under s.2(5) "shall make provision requiring each Health Board to submit … a scheme for the exercise of their functions", which the Ministers may approve with or without modifications, and may themselves make if a Board fails to do so (s.2(6)). Under **s.2(7)**, a Board "may at any time, **and if directed by the Secretary of State shall**, within such period as he may specify, submit a new scheme for the exercise of their functions". This is a discrete, expressly conferred power of direction over the Board's own scheme of delegation ([s.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/2)).

### 2.3 Direction on service failure — s.78A

Section 78A applies where it is a function of a body or person under the 1978 Act or the 2021 Act to provide or secure the provision of a service, and the Scottish Ministers consider that the body "has failed, is failing or is likely to fail" either to provide the service or "to provide it to a standard which they regard as acceptable" (s.78A(1)).

> **s.78A(2):** "The Scottish Ministers may, where they consider it necessary for the purpose of ensuring the provision of the service in question to a standard which they regard as acceptable, **direct that specified functions** of the body or person … **be performed, for a specified period and to a specified extent**, by— (a) a body falling within subsection (4), or (b) one or more persons falling within subsection (5)."
> ([s.78A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/78A))

Key features from the retrieved text:

- **The trigger is anticipatory and subjective.** "has failed, is failing or is **likely to fail**", and the standard is one "which **they regard** as acceptable" — the judgement is the Ministers' own.
- **Who may be directed to step in.** A Health Board, a Special Health Board, the Agency or HIS (s.78A(4)); or an employee of any of those bodies, a member of the staff of the Scottish Administration, or an employee of a local authority (s.78A(5)).
- **Compliance is compulsory.** "An appointed person **must comply** with a direction given under subsection (2)" (s.78A(7)).
- **Acts of the appointee are attributed to the failing body.** Anything done or omitted by the appointed person in performing the specified functions "is to be regarded as done or omitted by" the original body (s.78A(9)), and a third party dealing in good faith and for value need not inquire into the appointee's powers (s.78A(10)).
- **Cost falls on the failing body** unless the direction says otherwise (s.78A(8)).
- **The direction is revisable.** "The Scottish Ministers may vary or withdraw a direction given under subsection (2)" (s.78A(11)).

Note that s.78A is a power to **re-allocate the performance of functions**, not merely to instruct the failing body — it is the more intrusive of the two failure powers in operational terms, though it does not disturb the body's membership.

### 2.4 Default powers — s.77

Where the Ministers are of the opinion, "on representations made to him or otherwise", that a Health Board (or an NHS trust, the Dental Estimates Board, or HIS) has **failed to carry out any functions** conferred or imposed by or under the 1978 Act or the 2021 Act, "or have in carrying out those functions **failed to comply with any regulations, schemes, proposals or directions** relating to those functions", they may — **after holding an inquiry** — make an order declaring the body to be in default (s.77(1)).

The consequence is severe and automatic: "the members of the body shall **forthwith vacate their office**", and the order must provide for the appointment or election of new members and may authorise a person to act in the body's place in the interim (s.77(2)) ([s.77](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/77)).

Two points of contrast with s.78A:
- **Non-compliance with a direction is itself a default trigger.** Section 77(1) expressly reaches a failure "to comply with any … directions", which is the enforcement route standing behind s.2(5).
- **Section 77 carries a procedural precondition that s.78A does not** — an inquiry must be held first.

### 2.5 The general machinery for directions — s.105

Section 105 governs orders, regulations and directions under the Act. Two subsections bear directly on directions:

> **s.105(6):** "Where under any provision of this Act— (a) power to make an order may be exercisable, or **(b) directions may be given**, or (c) schemes may be made, that provision includes **power to vary or revoke** the order or direction or scheme … by subsequent order or subsequent directions or subsequent scheme made in the like manner and subject to the same provisions."
>
> **s.105(7):** "Any power conferred by this Act to make regulations, orders **or directions** may be exercised— (a) either in relation to **all cases** to which the power extends, or in those cases subject to exceptions, or in relation to any **specified cases or classes of case**, and (b) subject to such other exceptions or conditions as the Secretary of State thinks fit, and shall include power to make such supplementary, incidental, consequential, transitory, transitional or saving provision as appears … to be expedient."
> ([s.105](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/105))

Importantly, the statutory-instrument and parliamentary-procedure requirements in s.105(1)–(4) are directed at **orders and regulations**, not directions: s.105(1) makes "any power to make orders and regulations … exercisable by statutory instrument", and s.105(2) subjects statutory instruments to annulment. Directions are not brought within that machinery by the retrieved text. This is what makes the s.2(5) power administratively rapid — a direction requires no instrument and no parliamentary process.

### 2.6 Boundaries of the direction power — what the retrieved material does *not* support

- **Guidance is not direction.** Where the Act wants a "have regard" duty it says so: under **s.12IV**, HIS, every Health Board, every relevant Special Health Board and the Agency "must **have regard to** any guidance issued by the Scottish Ministers about the operation of sections 12IP to 12IU", with a duty to consult before issuing and to publish it ([s.12IV](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1978/29/section/12IV)). This is a materially weaker obligation than s.2(5) compliance, and the two should not be conflated.
- **Form and publication of s.2(5) directions.** The retrieved provisions do not state that a direction must be in writing or published. **The available database does not contain, in the material retrieved, any provision imposing a general form, notification or publication requirement on directions under the 1978 Act.** Any assertion that s.2(5) directions must be in writing should be verified before reliance.
- **Judicial control.** Case law on the limits of these powers is out of scope in this research mode and none was retrieved.

### 2.7 Why the provisions read "Secretary of State"

The 1978 Act is a pre-devolution enactment and its text still refers throughout to "the Secretary of State". Two provisions of the Scotland Act 1998 convert those references:

- **s.53(1)–(2)(c):** functions "conferred on a Minister of the Crown by any **pre-commencement enactment**" are, "so far as they are exercisable **within devolved competence**, … exercisable by the Scottish Ministers instead of by a Minister of the Crown". A "pre-commencement enactment" includes an Act passed before the Scotland Act 1998 (s.53(3)(a)), which the 1978 Act is ([Scotland Act 1998, s.53](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46/section/53)).
- **s.117:** so far as necessary for or in consequence of the exercise of a function by a member of the Scottish Government within devolved competence, any pre-commencement enactment "shall be read as if references to a Minister of the Crown (however described) were or included references to the Scottish Ministers" ([Scotland Act 1998, s.117](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/46/section/117)).

Consistently with this, the more recently inserted provisions of the 1978 Act — s.77's reference to HIS, s.78A and s.12IV — name "the Scottish Ministers" directly on their face.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The National Health Service (Scotland) Act 1978 (ukpga/1978/29) is recorded with extent `["United Kingdom"]` and the Scotland Act 1998 (ukpga/1998/46) likewise. Note that the *extent* of the 1978 Act as a UK Act is distinct from its **application**, which is to the health service in Scotland; the powers described operate on Scottish Health Boards only.
- **Status.** Both Acts are recorded as `revised` — i.e. the text held incorporates effected amendments. The retrieved s.2, s.77, s.78A, s.105 and s.12IV texts all show amendment (the "2021 Act" references in ss.2(5), 77(1) and 78A, and the s.12IV staffing provisions, are later insertions).
- **Devolved competence.** The transfer under Scotland Act 1998 s.53 operates only "so far as [the functions] are exercisable within devolved competence". Health is a devolved matter, but the qualification is on the face of the provision and is not disapplied by anything retrieved.
- **Caveat on section numbering.** The provision returned at `…/ukpga/1978/29/section/2CA` carries the internal heading "Section 2CB) … Functions of Health Boards outside Scotland". The URI and the text's own numbering disagree, so the citation for that provision should be checked against legislation.gov.uk directly before it is relied on. It is not material to the direction powers described above.
- **Currency.** The API output does not carry per-section commencement or "prospective" annotations, so the in-force position of the most recently inserted provisions should be confirmed against the relevant commencement instruments before reliance.
