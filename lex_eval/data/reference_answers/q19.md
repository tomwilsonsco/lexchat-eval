## 1. Question

> are there any requirements for the level of vitamins in margarine for sale in Scotland?

Legislation only. Case law is out of scope, so an answer that turns on a decided case is not expected to cite one here.

**Scope of this answer:** Covers the vitamin content requirements for margarine sold by retail in Scotland, the instrument that imposes them, and the offence and enforcement provisions attached. Limited to Scotland and to legislation.

## 2. Reference answer

<!-- BEGIN REFERENCE ANSWER -->

### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

Yes. The requirement is in **regulation 4 of the Spreadable Fats, Milk and Milk Products (Scotland) Regulations 2008** ([ssi/2008/216](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/4)).

Margarine sold by retail in Scotland must contain, in every 100 grams:

- **vitamin A:** not less than 800 and not more than 1,000 micrograms
- **vitamin D:** not less than 7.05 and not more than 8.82 micrograms

with a proportionate amount in any part of 100 grams. Contravention is an offence under regulation 6, enforced by food authorities under regulation 5.

The instrument to cite is the **2008** Scottish Regulations. The Spreadable Fats (Marketing Standards) (Scotland) Regulations 1999 are their revoked predecessor and are not the current source of the rule.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The requirement

> **Regulation 4 — Vitamin content of margarine**
> No person may sell by retail any margarine unless it contains in every 100 grams —
> (a) not less than 800 micrograms and not more than 1,000 micrograms of vitamin A; and
> (b) not less than 7.05 micrograms and not more than 8.82 micrograms of vitamin D,
> and a proportionate amount in any part of 100 grams.

([ssi/2008/216, reg 4](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/4))

The obligation is framed as a prohibition on **retail sale**, not on manufacture or import, so it bites at the point of sale to the consumer.

#### 2.2 Offence, enforcement and exemptions

- **Offence and penalty:** regulation 6 ([ssi/2008/216, reg 6](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/6)).
- **Enforcement:** regulation 5, by food authorities ([reg 5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/5)).
- **Exemptions:** regulation 3 ([reg 3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/3)).
- **Revocations and transitional provision:** regulations 8 and 9 ([reg 8](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2008/216/regulation/8)).

#### 2.3 The 1999 Regulations are the predecessor, not the current rule

The Spreadable Fats (Marketing Standards) (Scotland) Regulations 1999 contained a materially identical regulation 4 ([ssi/1999/34, reg 4](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/1999/34/regulation/4)), with its own offence, enforcement and revocation provisions. They were superseded by the 2008 Regulations, whose regulation 8 deals with revocations.

Because the figures are the same in both, an answer citing the 1999 Regulations will state the **right numbers from the wrong instrument**. That is a citation error rather than a substantive one, but it is the error to watch for here.

#### 2.4 Contrast with England

The equivalent English requirement was **removed in 2013**: regulation 3(4) of the Environmental Noise, Site Waste Management Plans and Spreadable Fats etc. (Revocations and Amendments) Regulations 2013 omitted regulation 4 of the England Regulations ([uksi/2013/2854, reg 3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2013/2854/regulation/3)). The Scottish requirement was untouched by that instrument. An answer that reasons from the English position to Scotland will get this backwards.
