## 2. Answer

# Can you identify duties or functions conferred on procurator fiscals, prosecutors, or the Lord Advocate under the Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014?

## Summary Answer (BLUF)

The Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014 confers duties on these office-holders in three
groups: **overarching duties** on the Lord Advocate (ss.1, 1A, 2), a set of **victims' rights
duties** (ss.3B to 3I, 9A to 9E), and two **prosecution-specific duties**, the duty to make rules
for reviewing a decision not to prosecute (s.4) and the duty to disclose information about criminal
proceedings (s.6).

Procurators fiscal are reached in two different ways, and the distinction matters: some duties bind
them because they fall within the **definition of "prosecutor"**, and others reach them through the
**interpretation provision in section 32**.

## Detailed Analysis

### Overarching duties on the Lord Advocate

- **s.1 (General principles)**: the Lord Advocate must have regard to the general principles when
  carrying out functions relating to victims and witnesses.
- **s.1A (Further general principles applicable to victims)**: further principles applying to
  victims specifically.
- **s.2 (Standards of service)**: the Lord Advocate must set and publish standards of service
  relating to the functions covered by the Act.

### Victims' rights duties

- **s.3B (The Victims' Code for Scotland)**.
- **s.3C (Victims' right to receive information)**.
- **s.3D (Referral to providers of victim support services)**.
- **s.3E (Victims' right to understand and be understood)**.
- **s.3F (Victims' right to interpretation and translation)**.
- **s.3H (Victims' right to reimbursement of expenses)**.
- **s.3I (Victims' right to return of property)**.
- **s.9A (Victims' right to protection during criminal investigations)**, **s.9B (individual
  assessment of victims)**, **s.9C (measures to assist victims)**.
- **s.9D (Right to avoid contact between victim and offender)**.
- **s.9E (Victims' right to protection of privacy)**.

### Prosecution-specific duties

- **s.4 (Rules: review of decision not to prosecute)**: the Lord Advocate must make and publish
  rules establishing a procedure by which a person may request a review of a decision not to
  prosecute.
- **s.6 (Disclosure of information about criminal proceedings)**: a duty to disclose information
  about criminal proceedings to a person who requests it.

**These two are distinct duties and must not be conflated.** Section 4 is about reviewing a decision
not to prosecute; section 6 is about disclosing information about proceedings. An answer that
attributes the review procedure to section 6 has the Act wrong.

### Other relevant provisions

- **s.5 (Restorative justice)**.
- **s.8A (Certain medical examinations: additional information to be provided)**.
- **s.18 (Review of arrangements for vulnerable witnesses)** and **s.23 (Victim statements)**.
- **s.27B (Provision of information to person supporting victim)**.
- **s.29B (Application of Act where victim's death caused by offence)**.

### How procurators fiscal are caught

Section 32 is the interpretation provision and is the key to reading the duties correctly. Rather
than naming procurators fiscal in every operative section, the Act reaches them partly through the
defined term "prosecutor" and partly through section 32's interpretation of how the victims' rights
provisions apply. An answer that lists duties without explaining this mechanism has not answered the
question as asked, because the question is specifically about procurators fiscal and prosecutors as
well as the Lord Advocate.

## Jurisdiction & Status

Scotland. The Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014 (asp 2014/1) extends to Scotland and is in
force, status "revised". It has been commenced in stages by a series of commencement orders,
including SSI 2020/405 (No. 7), SSI 2021/39 (No. 8) and SSI 2022/22 (No. 9).

## References

- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, ss.1, 1A, 2 (general principles, standards of service)
- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, ss.3B to 3I (Victims' Code and victims' rights)
- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, s.4 (rules: review of decision not to prosecute)
- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, s.6 (disclosure of information about criminal proceedings)
- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, ss.9A to 9E (protection, assessment, contact, privacy)
- Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014, s.32 (interpretation)

The points a correct answer has to make, most important first. These are the

1. Section 4 of the Victims and Witnesses (Scotland) Act 2014 requires the Lord Advocate to make and publish rules for reviewing a decision not to prosecute.
2. Section 6 of that Act imposes a duty to disclose information about criminal proceedings on request, which is a different duty from the section 4 review rules.
3. Section 1 requires the Lord Advocate to have regard to the general principles when carrying out functions relating to victims and witnesses, and section 2 requires standards of service to be set and published.
4. Sections 9D and 9E confer duties to enable a victim to avoid contact with the offender and to protect a victim's privacy.
5. Procurators fiscal are brought within these duties through the definition of prosecutor and the interpretation provision in section 32, rather than being named in each operative section.
