## 1. Question

> What is the process for making and reviewing an Integration Joint Board's strategic plan?

### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The process is governed by **Part 2 of the Public Bodies (Joint Working) (Scotland) Act 2014 (asp 9)**, ss.29–38. An integration joint board ("IJB") must prepare a strategic plan setting out the arrangements for carrying out the integration functions and how they will achieve the national health and wellbeing outcomes, divided by locality (**s.29**). Preparation runs through a **prescribed three-stage consultative sequence** with its strategic planning group and then a wider body of consultees — proposals, first draft, second draft — before the plan may be finalised (**s.33**), and it must be published together with a statement of the action taken (**s.35**). The board **must review the plan's effectiveness before the expiry of each three-year "relevant period"** (**s.37(1)(a), (10)**), having regard to the integration delivery principles and the national health and wellbeing outcomes and seeking the views of its strategic planning group (**s.37(2)**); it *may* review at any other time (**s.37(1)(b)**). Following a review it *may* prepare a replacement plan (**s.37(3)**), and the constituent authorities acting jointly *may direct* it to do so in the circumstances in **s.38**, with which it **must comply**.

---

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 Who is under the duty

The Act imposes the duty on the "integration authority" for the area of a local authority (**s.29(1)**). Under **s.59**, where functions are delegated under the integration model in s.1(4)(a), the integration authority **is the integration joint board** established under the scheme ([s.59](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/59)). The provisions below therefore apply to an IJB directly.

"**Constituent authority**" means each local authority and the Health Board which prepared the integration scheme in pursuance of which the IJB was, or is to be, established (**s.61**) ([s.61](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/61)). That definition matters at three points: information provision (s.34), consultation on the second draft (s.33(5)(a)), and the power to direct a replacement plan (s.38).

#### 2.2 The duty to prepare, and what the plan must contain (s.29)

A strategic plan is a document —
- setting out **the arrangements for carrying out the integration functions** for the local authority's area over the period of the plan (s.29(2)(a));
- setting out **how those arrangements are intended to achieve, or contribute to achieving, the national health and wellbeing outcomes** (s.29(2)(b)); and
- including such other material as the integration authority thinks fit (s.29(2)(c)).

The s.29(2)(a) provision **must** include provision dividing the local authority's area into **two or more localities** and setting out **separately** the arrangements for each locality (**s.29(3)**) ([s.29](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/29)).

Timing of the first plan: it must be prepared **before the integration start day** (s.29(5)), which for an IJB is the day functions are delegated to it (s.29(6)(a)). Where functions are to be delegated before the day prescribed under s.9(3), the first plan must specify the day on which functions are to be delegated (s.29(4)).

#### 2.3 What the board must have regard to (s.30, s.31, s.5)

When preparing a strategic plan the integration authority **must have regard to**:
- **the integration delivery principles** (s.30(2)(a), defined in s.31); and
- **the national health and wellbeing outcomes** (s.30(2)(b), made under s.5).

It must also have regard to the effect its proposed arrangements may have on services, facilities or resources used in relation to arrangements in **another** integration authority's strategic plan, whether already prepared or under preparation, where the periods overlap (**s.30(3)–(4)**) ([s.30](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/30)).

The **integration delivery principles** (s.31(1)) are that the main purpose of services provided in pursuance of integration functions is to improve service-users' wellbeing, and that so far as consistent with that purpose the services should so far as possible be integrated from the service-user's point of view; take account of the particular needs, characteristics and circumstances of different service-users and of service-users in different parts of the area; respect service-users' rights and dignity; take account of participation in the community; protect and improve safety; improve quality; be **planned and led locally in a way which is engaged with the community** (including service-users, those who look after them and those involved in providing health or social care); best anticipate needs and prevent them arising; and make the best use of available facilities, people and other resources ([s.31](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/31)).

The **national health and wellbeing outcomes** are prescribed by the Scottish Ministers by regulations under **s.5(1)**, after consulting each local authority, each Health Board, each IJB then established, and representatives of the eleven groups listed in s.5(3) ([s.5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/5)). *The specific outcomes regulations were not retrieved in this run — see 2.9.*

#### 2.4 The strategic planning group (s.32)

**Before preparing its first strategic plan**, the integration authority must establish a "strategic planning group" (s.32(1)). For an IJB, its membership comprises:
- at least one person nominated by the **Health Board** which is a constituent authority (s.32(1)(a)(i));
- at least one person nominated by the **local authority** (or, where two or more local authorities are constituent authorities, by those authorities) (s.32(1)(a)(ii)–(iii));
- **one person in respect of each prescribed group**, being a person the authority considers representative of that group (s.32(1)(d), with the groups prescribed under s.32(2)); and
- **such other persons as the integration authority considers appropriate** (s.32(1)(e)).

During the period of any strategic plan, the group **must also** include a person to represent the interests of **each locality** set out in the plan under s.29(3)(a) (**s.32(7)**); the authority decides who is suitable and selects the representative (s.32(8)), and may select one person for two or more localities (s.32(9)).

The authority determines the group's size and (so far as not set out in s.32) the processes for appointment, removal and replacement (s.32(3)), and its procedure (s.32(11)); it may pay expenses and allowances (s.32(12)). A constituent authority may remove and replace its own nominee (s.32(5)); a member may resign at any time (s.32(6)); and **a vacancy does not affect the validity of anything the group does** (s.32(10)) ([s.32](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/32)).

#### 2.5 The staged preparation procedure (s.33) — the core of the process

Section 33 prescribes a **sequential, three-round process**. The statutory language is directive ("is to", "must"), and each round must take account of the previous one:

| Stage | What the board does | Who is consulted | Statutory basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Prepare **proposals for what the plan should contain**, and seek views on them | Strategic planning group | s.33(2) |
| 2 | Taking account of stage 1 views, prepare a **first draft** and seek views on it | Strategic planning group | s.33(3) |
| 3 | Taking account of stage 2 views, prepare a **second draft**, send a copy to the s.33(5) persons and to such others as it considers appropriate, and invite views within such period as it considers appropriate | Each constituent authority; representatives of each prescribed group; others as it considers appropriate | s.33(4) |
| 4 | **Finalise** the plan, taking account of the stage 3 views | — | s.33(7) |

The persons who **must** receive the second draft, where the integration authority is an IJB, are **each constituent authority** (s.33(5)(a)) and **persons the authority considers representative of each of the prescribed groups** (s.33(5)(d)) ([s.33](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/33)).

Two features are worth noting for compliance purposes:
- The obligation at stages 1–3 is to **seek views**; the obligation to **take account of** them is expressed separately at the start of each following stage and, for the final round, in s.33(7). Seeking views without demonstrably taking account of them would not satisfy the section.
- The consultation **period** at stage 3 is at the board's discretion ("within such period as the integration authority considers appropriate") — the Act sets no minimum.

#### 2.6 Information from constituent authorities (s.34)

A constituent authority **must** provide an IJB with such information as the board **may reasonably require** for the purpose of preparing a strategic plan (**s.34(1)**) ([s.34](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/34)). The equivalent duty applies on review under s.37(5).

#### 2.7 Publication (s.35)

**As soon as practicable after the finalisation of the plan under s.33**, the integration authority must publish its strategic plan (s.35(1)); and **at the same time** it must also publish **a statement of the action which it took in pursuance of s.33** (s.35(2)) ([s.35](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/35)). The second limb is an auditable record of the consultative process, not an optional annex.

#### 2.8 Review and replacement

**(a) Periodic review — s.37(1)(a) and (10).** The integration authority **must, before the expiry of the relevant period, review the effectiveness of its strategic plan**. "Relevant period" means the period of **3 years** beginning with the integration start day, and each subsequent period of 3 years beginning with:
- where a replacement plan is prepared following a review, **the day specified under s.37(9)** (the day the new plan's period begins); or
- where **no** replacement plan is prepared following a review, **the day on which the authority decides not to prepare one** (s.37(10)(b)).

So the three-year clock restarts from the decision point, not from the review's commencement. The authority **may** also carry out a review from time to time (s.37(1)(b)).

**(b) What a review must involve — s.37(2).** The authority must have regard to the integration delivery principles and the national health and wellbeing outcomes (s.37(2)(a)), and must **seek and have regard to the views of its strategic planning group** on (i) the effectiveness of the arrangements for carrying out the integration functions in the area, and (ii) **whether the authority should prepare a replacement strategic plan** (s.37(2)(b)). Subject to that, **the process of a review is such as the integration authority determines** (s.37(4)) — the Act prescribes the inputs, not the method.

**(c) Information on review — s.37(5)–(7).** The s.34 duty is mirrored: a constituent authority must give an IJB such information as it may reasonably require for the purpose of carrying out a review.

**(d) Replacement following review — s.37(3), (8), (9).** Following a review the authority **may** prepare a replacement plan. Where it does, **ss.30, 33, 34 and 35 apply to it as they apply to a s.29 plan** (s.37(8)) — i.e. the full considerations, three-stage consultation, information and publication requirements are repeated. The replacement plan **must specify a day on which the period of the plan is to begin** (s.37(9)) ([s.37](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/37)).

**(e) Directed replacement — s.38.** This section applies only where the integration authority **is an IJB** (s.38(1)). Where it appears to a constituent authority that the strategic plan **is preventing, or is likely to prevent, that authority from carrying out any of its functions appropriately**, or in a way which complies with the integration delivery principles and contributes to achieving the national health and wellbeing outcomes, **the constituent authorities acting jointly may direct** the IJB to prepare a replacement strategic plan (s.38(2)).

The direction:
- **must be in writing** and **include a statement summarising the reasons** for giving it (s.38(3));
- **must specify** a day by which the replacement plan must be prepared and a day on which the plan's period is to begin (s.38(4)), which the constituent authorities acting jointly may later substitute by further direction (s.38(5)); and
- **must be complied with** — "An integration authority must comply with a direction given to it under subsection (2)" (s.38(6)).

Again, **ss.30, 33, 34 and 35 apply** to a plan prepared under a s.38 direction (s.38(7)) ([s.38](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/38)).

Note the constitutional shape of s.38: the power is exercisable **only by the constituent authorities acting jointly**, not by either alone and not by the Scottish Ministers. The trigger is the effect of the plan on a **constituent authority's own functions**, not a general view that the plan is deficient.

**(f) Significant decisions taken outside the plan — s.36.** Where an integration authority proposes a significant decision about the arrangements for carrying out integration functions and intends it to take effect **other than by revising its strategic plan under s.37**, it must seek and have regard to the views of its strategic planning group and take such action as it thinks fit to secure that **users of the service** are involved in and consulted on the decision (s.36(3)–(4)). "Significant decision" means one the authority considers **might significantly affect the provision of a service** provided in pursuance of the integration functions in the area (s.36(2)) ([s.36](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2014/9/section/36)). This closes the obvious gap — it prevents the consultative regime being bypassed by taking major decisions outside the plan.

#### 2.9 The prescribed consultees (SSI 2014/283)

The groups prescribed for the purposes of **s.33(6)** — i.e. those whose representatives must receive the second draft under s.33(5)(d) — are, subject to reg 5, **the "standard consultees"** (reg 3) ([SSI 2014/283, reg 3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/283/regulation/3)).

The **standard consultees** are listed in the Schedule: health professionals; users of health care; carers of users of health care; commercial providers of health care; non-commercial providers of health care; social care professionals; users of social care; carers of users of social care; commercial providers of social care; non-commercial providers of social care; staff of the Health Board and local authority who are not health or social care professionals; non-commercial providers of social housing; and third sector bodies carrying out activities related to health or social care ([SSI 2014/283, Schedule](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/283/schedule)). "Third sector" includes representative groups, interest groups, social enterprises and community organisations (reg 1(2)).

**Regulation 5** imposes a locality/connection qualification: users must reside in the area of the local authority which prepared the integration scheme **and** use services provided in pursuance of integration functions; carers must care for such a user; non-commercial providers of social housing and third sector bodies must operate in that area; and in any other case the consultee must operate in that area **and** provide, or be engaged in relation to, services provided in pursuance of integration functions ([SSI 2014/283, reg 5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2014/283/regulation/5)).

**Two gaps in the retrieved material, stated rather than filled:**
- **Section 32(2) (strategic planning group membership) does not appear to be prescribed for by SSI 2014/283.** The Regulations expressly prescribe for ss.6(2)(a) and 46(4)(a) (reg 2), s.33(6) (reg 3) and s.41(4) (reg 4). **The available database does not contain, in the material retrieved, the instrument prescribing the groups for the purposes of s.32(2)**; the groups whose representatives must sit on the strategic planning group under s.32(1)(d) should therefore be confirmed against the current subordinate legislation before reliance.

### 6.1 Research plan

1. **Identify the governing Act and the duty to prepare a strategic plan**
   Identify the primary Scottish legislation establishing integration joint boards and requiring them to prepare strategic plans, and set out what a strategic plan must contain and the period it covers.

2. **Establish the matters the board must consider when preparing a plan**
   Find the provisions setting out what the integration joint board must have regard to when preparing a strategic plan, including any statutory principles and any prescribed national outcomes, and any duty to consider the effect on neighbouring areas.

3. **Set out the consultative body and the staged preparation procedure**
   Find the provisions establishing the strategic planning group — its composition and how members are appointed and removed — and the sequence of proposals, drafts and consultations the board must follow before the plan can be finalised.

4. **Establish the publication requirements and any duty to provide information**
