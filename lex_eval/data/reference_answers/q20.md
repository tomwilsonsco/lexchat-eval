## 2. Answer

# Whether sections 35A and 35ZA of the Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009 can run concurrently

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**Yes, they can run concurrently.**

Both sections are drafted as preconditions of the **same event**, the laying of a climate change plan under section 35. Neither is expressed as a precondition of the other, and neither contains any language of displacement or sequencing that would require one procedure to finish before the other begins.

- **Section 35ZA** requires a public consultation of **no less than 6 months** to inform the proposals and policies to be set out in the plan, with a statement laid before the Parliament first.
- **Section 35A** requires a **draft** of the plan to be laid before the Parliament for **120 days**, of which no fewer than 60 must be sitting days.

---

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 Section 35ZA, the consultation stage

> **Section 35ZA - Preparation of climate change plan: public consultation**
> (1) The Scottish Ministers must carry out a public consultation to inform the proposals and policies to be set out in a climate change plan laid under section 35(1)(b).
> (2) Before carrying out a consultation under subsection (1), the Scottish Ministers must lay a statement before the Parliament setting out -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the methodologies the Scottish Ministers plan to use to consult ...
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) the period of time for which the Scottish Ministers plan to consult, **which must be a period no less than 6 months**,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) such other information as the Scottish Ministers consider appropriate.

([asp/2009/12, s.35ZA](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2009/12/section/35ZA))

The consultation is tied to the plan **"laid under section 35(1)(b)"**, that is, to the final laying. It is not tied to the section 35A draft.

### 2.2 Section 35A, the draft scrutiny stage

> **Section 35A - Climate change plan: further procedure**
> (1) The Scottish Ministers must, **before laying a climate change plan under section 35** before the Scottish Parliament -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) lay a draft of the plan before the Parliament for a period of 120 days, of which no fewer than 60 must be days on which the Parliament is not dissolved or in recess,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(aa) request the views of such persons as they consider appropriate with [specified expertise],
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) have regard to - (i) any representations on the draft plan made to them, (ii) any views ... (iii) any resolution relating to the draft plan passed by the Parliament, and (iv) any report relating to the draft plan published by any committee ...

([asp/2009/12, s.35A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2009/12/section/35A))

Subsection (1A) additionally requires Ministers to publish a response to any such resolution or committee report.

### 2.3 Why they can run together

**Both are preconditions of the same act.** Section 35ZA(1) attaches to the plan "laid under section 35(1)(b)"; section 35A(1) attaches to the moment "before laying a climate change plan under section 35". The gateway each guards is the laying of the plan, not the other procedure.

**Neither refers to the other.** Section 35A does not require the section 35ZA consultation to have concluded, and section 35ZA does not require the section 35A draft period to be complete or not yet begun. Neither contains "after", "once", or any comparable sequencing word directed at the other section.

**Nothing in the subject matter forces an order.** Section 35ZA informs "the proposals and policies to be set out in" the plan, and section 35A subjects a draft to parliamentary scrutiny and requires Ministers to have regard to representations. Both are inputs to the same final document, and Ministers can be receiving consultation responses and parliamentary representations over overlapping periods, having regard to both when settling the plan.

**The practical reading.** Running the 6-month consultation and the 120-day draft period concurrently is permissible on the text. What Ministers cannot do is lay the plan under section 35 before both have been completed, because each is independently a precondition of that act.

### 2.4 The contrary reading, and why it does not hold

It can be argued that a consultation which "informs the proposals and policies to be set out in" the plan must logically precede a draft that already contains those proposals and policies, so the two must be sequential. That reasoning has intuitive force but no textual anchor: it derives a mandatory order from the subject matter rather than from words in either section, and section 35A(1)(b) expressly contemplates Ministers continuing to receive and have regard to input after the draft is laid. An answer that concludes the sections must be sequential is reading in a restriction the Act does not impose.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Climate Change (Scotland) Act 2009 extends to Scotland. Both sections were returned as current revised text.
- **Insertion history.** Both sections are later insertions into the 2009 Act, which is why they carry the 35ZA and 35A numbering. Which interpretation regime governs the Act as a result is addressed separately, in the answer to question 15.
- **Limit of this answer.** This is a question of construction on which the text does not speak directly. The conclusion rests on the absence of sequencing language plus the shared gateway in section 35, which is a strong but not conclusive basis.
