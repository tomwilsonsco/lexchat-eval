### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**The complete list cannot be produced with the available legislation tools, and the correct answer says so.**

The question asks for an intersection of two sets: instruments **made under section 95** of the Social Security (Scotland) Act 2018, and instruments whose text **contains a £ symbol**. The second set is testable, because instrument text is retrievable. The first is not, for a specific and checkable reason:

> **The preamble that recites the powers exercised ("in exercise of the powers conferred by section 95...") is not part of the retrievable text of an instrument.** Retrieved text begins at section 1. No search can therefore find instruments by their enabling power.

So the population cannot be enumerated, and an answer that offers a list as if it were complete is misrepresenting the search it ran. The reviewers put the population at **33 instruments**; that figure comes from a source other than these tools.

One member of the intersection can be confirmed by direct retrieval: **SSI 2024/311** was made under section 95 and its text contains the £ symbol.

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 What section 95 actually is

> **Section 95 - Ancillary provision**
> The Scottish Ministers may by regulations make any **incidental, supplementary, consequential, transitional, transitory or saving provision** they consider appropriate for the purposes of, or in connection with, or for giving full effect to this Act or any provision made under it.

([asp/2018/9, s.95](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2018/9/section/95))

This shapes the whole question, and it is the first thing a correct answer should establish. Section 95 is **not** a subject-matter power. It does not authorise a scheme of assistance, so no instrument made under it has "section 95" or anything like it in its title. It is the general tidying-up power, exercised alongside other powers whenever an instrument needs consequential amendments, revocations or savings.

Two consequences follow:

1. Instruments made under section 95 are almost always made under **several** powers at once, with section 95 supplying only the ancillary provisions. They are not "section 95 instruments" in any natural sense.
2. They are scattered across every subject area of the Act, so **no topic search will collect them**. They have nothing in common except the ancillary power.

This also explains why the population is as large as 33: an ancillary power is exercised routinely over the life of a major Act.

#### 2.2 Why the population cannot be retrieved

The decisive point is what the tools return. Retrieving an instrument's text gives the operative provisions from section 1 onwards. It does **not** give the preamble. Checked against three instruments:

| Instrument | Text retrieved | Preamble reciting enabling powers |
|---|---|---|
| SSI 2024/311 | ~30,000 characters, from "Section 1) Citation and commencement" | **Absent** |
| SSI 2025/105 | Present, from section 1 | **Absent** |
| SSI 2025/119 | "No text content available" | **Absent** |

The enabling power is recorded only in that missing preamble. Section searching does not help either: it searches provision text, which likewise starts at section 1.

This is a **tool limitation, not a gap in the law**. The information exists on legislation.gov.uk; it is simply not in what these tools return. That distinction belongs in the answer, because it tells the user where to go instead rather than leaving them thinking the question is unanswerable in principle.

#### 2.3 What can be confirmed

**SSI 2024/311**, the Disability Assistance (Scottish Adult Disability Living Allowance) (Consequential Amendment, Revocation and Saving Provision) Regulations 2024, was made under section 95 and **its text contains the £ symbol**. Its title is itself the signature of an ancillary-power instrument: "Consequential Amendment, Revocation and Saving Provision" tracks the language of section 95 almost word for word.

Its opening provision:

> **Section 1 - Citation and commencement**
> These Regulations may be cited as the Disability Assistance (Scottish Adult Disability Living Allowance) (Consequential Amendment, Revocation and Saving Provision) Regulations 2024 and come into force on 21 March 2025.

The instrument amends the Social Security (Invalid Care Allowance) Regulations 1976, the Council Tax (Discounts) (Scotland) Regulations 1992 and others, in each case consequentially on the introduction of Scottish adult disability living allowance under section 31 of the 2018 Act. That is exactly the shape section 95 exists to authorise.

Confirming one member of a set is not enumerating the set. It shows the answer is not empty; it does not make it complete.

#### 2.4 What a correct answer looks like

- **Correct.** Explain that section 95 is the ancillary provision power; explain that instruments cannot be searched by enabling power because the preamble is not retrievable; give any instruments that can be confirmed, flagged as examples rather than a complete list; point the user to the legislation.gov.uk "enabling power" listing.
- **Also acceptable.** State plainly that the complete population cannot be established and stop.
- **Wrong.** Present a list of instruments as the answer without evidence that each was made under section 95. This is the same failure as question 13 inverted: there, no instrument existed and three were invented; here, 33 exist and none can be found.
- **Also wrong.** Exhaust the tool-call budget searching, then return nothing without explaining why the search could not succeed.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** Scotland. `asp/2018/9`, Social Security (Scotland) Act 2018, held in revised form.
- **The £ criterion.** Testable only once an instrument is identified. It cannot be used to narrow a search, because the tools do not offer full-text search across instruments.
- **Population size.** The figure of 33 instruments is the reviewers' count and is not derived from the retrieval here. It is recorded to show the scale of what is being missed, not cited as a retrieved fact.
- **Not addressed.** How many of the 33 contain the £ symbol is not stated. Only SSI 2024/311 was retrieved and checked.
