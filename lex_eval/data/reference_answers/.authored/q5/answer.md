# Appointment of the chair of Food Standards Scotland

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The chair is appointed by **the Scottish Ministers** under **section 6(1)(a) of the Food (Scotland) Act 2015 (asp 1)**: Food Standards Scotland consists of "a person appointed by the Scottish Ministers to chair Food Standards Scotland" together with **no fewer than 3 nor more than 7** other members, also appointed by the Scottish Ministers. The Act imposes **no requirement for Scottish Parliament approval or confirmation**, and **fixes no term of office** — membership is "for such period and on such terms and conditions as the Scottish Ministers may determine" (s.6(5)), subject to the early-ending provisions in ss.7 and 18. The appointment is nevertheless a **regulated public appointment**: s.17(4) of the 2015 Act inserted Food Standards Scotland into schedule 2 to the **Public Appointments and Public Bodies etc. (Scotland) Act 2003 (asp 4)**, so the process is governed by the **code of practice** prepared and published by the **Commissioner for Ethical Standards in Public Life in Scotland** under s.2(1) of that Act — and where the Commissioner considers the code has not been complied with in a material regard, the Commissioner **may direct the Scottish Ministers to delay making the appointment** until Parliament has considered the case, and **the Ministers must comply** (s.2(8)).

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 The body and the appointing power

**Section 1** establishes "a body corporate called Food Standards Scotland or, in Gaelic, Inbhe-Bidhe Alba" ([s.1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/1)).

> **s.6(1):** "Food Standards Scotland is to consist of— (a) **a person appointed by the Scottish Ministers to chair Food Standards Scotland**, and (b) no fewer than 3 nor more than 7 other members appointed by the Scottish Ministers."
> ([s.6](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/6))

Three points follow directly from the text:

1. **The appointing authority is the Scottish Ministers alone.** No approval, confirmation, nomination or veto by the Scottish Parliament, by a committee, or by any other body appears on the face of s.6.
2. **The chair is a distinct appointment, not an office filled from among the members.** Section 6(1)(a) creates a separate limb from s.6(1)(b), and the board size is "3 to 7 **other** members" — so the full board is between 4 and 8 people including the chair.
3. **The chair is appointed "to chair" the body**, i.e. appointed directly to that role rather than appointed as a member and subsequently elected chair.

### 2.2 Disqualification from appointment — s.6(2)

A person **may not be appointed** as a member if the person is:
- a member of the **Scottish Parliament**;
- a member of the **House of Commons**;
- a member of the **European Parliament**;
- an **office-holder in the Scottish Administration**;
- a **councillor** of any local authority; or
- an **employee** of any local authority.

The list applies to "a member", which by s.6(1) includes the chairing member.

### 2.3 The manner of appointment — s.6(3)–(4)

> **s.6(3):** "The Scottish Ministers **must** make appointments of members of Food Standards Scotland in a manner which encourages **equal opportunities** and in particular the observance of the **equal opportunity requirements**."

"Equal opportunities" and "equal opportunity requirements" bear the meanings given in **Section L2 of Part 2 of Schedule 5 to the Scotland Act 1998** (s.6(4)). This is a duty on the *process* of appointment, not merely on the outcome, and it is expressed as an obligation ("must").

### 2.4 Term, terms and conditions, remuneration — ss.6(5)–(7), 8

- **No statutory term.** "Membership is (subject to sections 7 and 18) for such period and on such terms and conditions as the Scottish Ministers may determine" (**s.6(5)**). The Act sets no minimum or maximum term for the chair; the period is set by the Ministers in the individual appointment.
- **Resignation.** A member may resign "by informing the Scottish Ministers in writing to that effect" (**s.6(6)**).
- **Re-appointment.** "A person may be re-appointed on, or after, ceasing to be a member" (**s.6(7)**). There is no statutory cap on the number of terms.
- **Remuneration.** A member is entitled to such remuneration as the Scottish Ministers may determine, and to such sums as they determine to reimburse or compensate for expenses properly incurred in exercising the member's functions; **it is for Food Standards Scotland to pay** those sums (**s.8**) ([s.8](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/8)). So the amount is set by the Ministers but borne by the body.

### 2.5 Deputising and a vacant chair — s.6(8)–(9)

The Scottish Ministers may assign to one of the **s.6(1)(b) members**:
- **(a)** the function of **deputising** for the chair; and
- **(b)** the function of **chairing Food Standards Scotland during any period when there is no person appointed under s.6(1)(a)** — i.e. an acting chair during a vacancy.

Such an assignment runs for the period the Ministers specify, may be resigned by written notice to the Ministers, and is made and ended in accordance with terms and conditions the Ministers determine (s.6(9)).

Note the constraint: the deputy or acting chair must be an **existing member appointed under s.6(1)(b)**. The Ministers cannot use s.6(8) to install an outsider as acting chair.

### 2.6 How the appointment ends early — ss.7 and 18

**Automatic termination (s.7(1)).** Membership **ends** if the person *becomes* any of the six disqualifying categories in s.6(2) — MSP, MP, MEP, office-holder in the Scottish Administration, councillor, or local authority employee. This is automatic; no ministerial act is required ([s.7](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/7)).

**Discretionary removal (s.7(2)).** The Scottish Ministers **may** end a person's membership if:
- **(a)** the person becomes an **undischarged bankrupt**; or
- **(b)** the Ministers are satisfied that the person —
  - **(i)** has, **without the permission of Food Standards Scotland**, been absent from its meetings for a period longer than **6 consecutive months**,
  - **(ii)** is **unable** to perform the functions of a member, or
  - **(iii)** is **unsuitable** to continue as a member.

Two features are worth noting: the absence ground turns on the *body's* permission, not the Ministers'; and grounds (ii) and (iii) are framed on the Ministers' own satisfaction, with "unsuitable" undefined in the retrieved text.

**Collective removal for serious failure (s.18).** Where the Scottish Ministers consider there has been a **serious failure** by Food Standards Scotland to exercise any of its functions, they may give it such directions as they consider appropriate to remedy the failure, having first **consulted** the body (s.18(1)–(3)). Such a direction **must be in writing** and **must include a statement summarising the reasons** for it (s.18(4)), and the Ministers **must publish** it (s.18(5)). If the body fails to comply, the Ministers may give effect to the direction themselves, exercising any of the body's functions in its place (s.18(6)), and **may end the membership of all its members** and, until new members are appointed, exercise any of its functions in its place or appoint another person to do so (s.18(7)) ([s.18](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/18)).

Section 18(7) is the only route in the retrieved material by which the chair's membership can be ended other than under s.7 — and it operates on the whole board, not on the chair individually.

### 2.7 The appointment is a *regulated* public appointment — s.17(4)

Section 17 of the 2015 Act applied a series of public-body statutes to Food Standards Scotland by amendment. The one that governs the appointment process is **s.17(4)**:

> "In the **Public Appointments and Public Bodies etc. (Scotland) Act 2003**, in **schedule 2 (specified authorities)**, after the entry relating to the Crofting Commission insert— 'Food Standards Scotland'."
> ([s.17](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2015/1/section/17))

This is confirmed by the current text of the 2003 Act: **Food Standards Scotland appears in the retrieved schedule 2** ([PAPB (Scotland) Act 2003, schedule 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2003/4/schedule/2)).

(Section 17 also brought the body within the Ethical Standards in Public Life etc. (Scotland) Act 2000 schedule 3, the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman Act 2002 schedule 2, the Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002 schedule 1, the Public Services Reform (Scotland) Act 2010 schedules 8, 19 and 20, the Public Records (Scotland) Act 2011, the Regulatory Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 schedule 1 and the Procurement Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 — none of which bears directly on the appointment process.)

### 2.8 What the regulated regime requires — PAPB (Scotland) Act 2003, s.2

Because Food Standards Scotland is a specified authority, the Ministers' appointment of its chair is subject to the statutory code regime ([PAPB (Scotland) Act 2003, s.2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2003/4/section/2)):

- **The code of practice (s.2(1)).** The **Commissioner for Ethical Standards in Public Life in Scotland** "is to prepare and publish a **code of practice** in respect of the making by the Scottish Ministers of any appointment or … recommendation for any appointment … to a body specified in schedule 2".
- **Its content (s.2(2)).** The code is to include guidelines as to the methods and practices to be employed, and may in particular cover **how vacancies are publicised**, **how applications are encouraged**, and **the basis on which the Ministers are to consider persons** for appointment.
- **Consultation on the code (s.2(4)).** In preparing or revising it the Commissioner must consult the Parliament and the Scottish Ministers, and invite representations from others.
- **Oversight (s.2(5)).** The Commissioner is to examine the Ministers' methods and practices in making such appointments, may examine the making of any particular appointment, and **is to investigate complaints** arising from an appointment.
- **Guidance (s.2(6)).** The Commissioner may issue guidance to the Ministers, generally or in a particular case, as to compliance with the code.
- **Enforcement (s.2(7)–(8)).** Where it appears to the Commissioner that the code **has not been complied with in a material regard**, the Commissioner has intimated that to the Ministers, and the Commissioner considers the code is unlikely to be complied with within a reasonable time (or, after a reasonable time, remains uncomplied with), the Commissioner **must report the case to the Parliament** and — **if the appointment has not yet been made** — **may direct the Scottish Ministers to delay making it until the Parliament has considered the case**. "**and the Scottish Ministers must comply with any such direction**."
- **The governing objective (s.2(9)).** The Commissioner is to exercise these functions with a view to ensuring appointments are **made fairly and openly**, and that so far as reasonably practicable **all categories of person** are afforded an opportunity to be considered.
- **Equal opportunities strategy (s.2(10)).** The Commissioner must prepare and publish a strategy for ensuring appointments are made in a manner encouraging equal opportunities, and may set targets — mirroring the duty imposed directly on the Ministers by s.6(3) of the 2015 Act.

The Ministers must also provide the Commissioner with such information as the Commissioner reasonably requires (**s.3(1)**), and may by order amend schedule 2 or adjust the Commissioner's functions in relation to appointments (**s.3(2)**), after consulting the Parliamentary corporation where the change is to the Commissioner's functions (s.3(2A)) ([s.3](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2003/4/section/3)).

### 2.9 What the retrieved material does *not* establish

- **The content of the code of practice.** The code is made under s.2(1) but is **not itself legislation** and was not retrieved. The practical mechanics of a chair appointment — advertisement, selection panel composition, interview, ministerial choice from a list of appointable candidates — are matters for the code, not the Acts. **The available database does not contain the code of practice**, and its requirements should be taken from the Commissioner's own published version.
- **Any prescribed term length.** No provision fixing the chair's term was retrieved; s.6(5) leaves it to the Ministers.
- **Any parliamentary confirmation requirement.** None appears in the retrieved text of the 2015 Act. The only parliamentary involvement in the retrieved material is the Commissioner's report to the Parliament under s.2(8) of the 2003 Act, which arises only on material non-compliance with the code.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Food (Scotland) Act 2015 (asp/2015/1) and the Public Appointments and Public Bodies etc. (Scotland) Act 2003 (asp/2003/4) are both recorded with extent `["Scotland"]`. The regime applies to Scotland only.
- **Status.** Both Acts are recorded as `revised` — the text held incorporates effected amendments. The 2003 Act text shows amendment: s.2(1) now refers to the **Commissioner for Ethical Standards in Public Life in Scotland** (the office having replaced the former Commissioner for Public Appointments in Scotland named in the s.1 heading), and s.3(2A) is a later insertion.
- **A drafting artefact in the retrieved 2003 Act.** The section retrieved at `…/asp/2003/4/section/1` is titled "Commissioner for Public Appointments in Scotland" while s.2(1) and the s.20 interpretation provision both use "Commissioner for Ethical Standards in Public Life in Scotland". The latter is the operative name; the heading appears not to have been updated. This does not affect the substance.
- **Commencement.** The Food (Scotland) Act 2015 was commenced by the Food (Scotland) Act 2015 (Commencement) Order 2015 (ssi/2015/99), identified in the search results but not retrieved at section level. The API output carries no per-section commencement annotation, so the in-force date of any particular provision should be confirmed against that Order before reliance.
- **Schedule 2 is amendable by order.** Under s.3(2)(a) of the 2003 Act the Scottish Ministers may amend schedule 2 by order. The regulated status of Food Standards Scotland should therefore be confirmed against the current text rather than assumed from s.17(4) of the 2015 Act alone — although the schedule 2 text retrieved in this run does list it.
