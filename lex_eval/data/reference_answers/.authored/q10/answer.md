### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

Section 126(6) of the Scotland Act 1998 defines the Scottish Administration as **the office-holders in the Scottish Administration and the members of the staff of the Scottish Administration**.

The office-holders divide in two under section 126(7)(a): **ministerial** (members of the Scottish Government and junior Scottish Ministers) and **non-ministerial**.

The non-ministerial offices are where the difficulty lies. Section 126(8) names **three** expressly, then adds "any other office of a description specified in an **Order in Council**".

Separately, **Orders under section 104** of the Act have declared whole bodies to be part of the Scottish Administration. Six were found: the Scottish Court Service, the Scottish Housing Regulator, Revenue Scotland, Food Standards Scotland, the Scottish Fiscal Commission and Consumer Scotland. These are not Orders in Council under section 126(8)(b), so a search confined to that route misses them.

**A complete list therefore cannot be produced from primary legislation alone.** It requires collecting every Order in Council made under section 126(8)(b) and section 104 Orders, and an answer that offers a list without having done so should say the list is incomplete rather than present it as exhaustive.

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The definition

> **Section 126 - Interpretation**
> (6) References in this Act and in any other enactment to the Scottish Administration are to **the office-holders in the Scottish Administration and the members of the staff of the Scottish Administration**.
> (7) For the purposes of this Act -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) references to office-holders in the Scottish Administration are to -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(i) members of the Scottish Government and junior Scottish Ministers, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) the holders of offices in the Scottish Administration which are not ministerial offices, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) references to members of the staff of the Scottish Administration are to the staff of the persons referred to in paragraph (a).
> (8) For the purposes of this Act, the offices in the Scottish Administration which are not ministerial offices are -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the Registrar General of Births, Deaths and Marriages for Scotland, the Keeper of the Registers of Scotland and the Keeper of the Records of Scotland, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **any other office of a description specified in an Order in Council** ...

([ukpga/1998/46, s.126](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/126))

Note that the Scottish Administration is defined as a body of **persons**, office-holders and staff, not as a set of organisations. A question phrased in terms of "organisations, offices etc." maps onto the office-holders limb.

Related provisions: section 44 constitutes the Scottish Government ([s.44](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/44)), section 48 deals with the Scottish Law Officers ([s.48](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/48)), and section 51 with the Civil Service ([s.51](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/51)).

#### 2.2 The three offices named in the Act

Section 126(8)(a) names, exhaustively for that paragraph:

1. the Registrar General of Births, Deaths and Marriages for Scotland
2. the Keeper of the Registers of Scotland
3. the Keeper of the Records of Scotland

#### 2.3 The open-ended limb: Orders in Council under section 126(8)(b)

Section 126(8)(b) is the reason the list cannot be closed from the Act. Offices are added by Order in Council, each specifying further offices. Four were retrieved:

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 1999** ([uksi/1999/1127, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/1999/1127/article/2)) specifies the offices of **procurator fiscal and procurator fiscal depute**, and then every office listed in column 1 of its Schedule. The Schedule itself was not returned by the research tools, so the offices it lists are not stated here.

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2012** ([uksi/2012/3073, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2012/3073/article/2)):

> The following offices are specified for the purposes of section 126(8)(b) of the Scotland Act 1998 ... -
> (a) Her Majesty's inspector of constabulary appointed under section 71(2) of the Police and Fire Reform (Scotland) Act 2012;
> (b) Her Majesty's Chief Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...;
> (c) Her Majesty's Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...; and
> (d) Assistant Inspector of the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service ...

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2015** ([uksi/2015/200, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2015/200/article/2)):

> ... (a) the Clerk of the Sheriff Appeal Court appointed under section 59(1) of the Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014;
> (b) a Deputy Clerk of the Sheriff Appeal Court appointed under section 60(1) of that Act.

**The Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2018** ([uksi/2018/781, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2018/781/article/2)):

> ... (a) the Auditor of the Court of Session appointed under section 14(3) of the Civil Litigation (Expenses and Group Proceedings) (Scotland) Act 2018;
> (b) the auditor of the Sheriff Appeal Court appointed under that section;
> (c) the auditor of the sheriff court appointed under that section.

The 2012 Order also contains its own revocation provisions (articles 3 and 4): it removes the constabulary inspector entries from the 1999 Schedule and revokes the Scottish Administration (Offices) Order 2005 ([uksi/2005/1467, art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2005/1467/article/2)), which had specified the fire inspectors. The set of specified offices changes over time: later Orders both add and remove.

#### 2.4 Bodies made part of the Scottish Administration by section 104 Orders

Section 104 lets subordinate legislation make "such provision as the person making the legislation considers necessary or expedient in consequence of any provision made by or under any Act of the Scottish Parliament" ([ukpga/1998/46, s.104](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/1998/46/section/104)). Orders made on that footing, following the Act of the Scottish Parliament that created each body, declare the body itself to be part of the Scottish Administration, and provide that references to an office-holder in the Scottish Administration include it. They do not specify an office under section 126(8)(b).

| Body | Order | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Scottish Court Service | Judiciary and Courts (Scotland) Act 2008 (Consequential Provisions and Modifications) Order 2009 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2009/2231/article/2) |
| Scottish Housing Regulator | Housing (Scotland) Act 2010 (Consequential Provisions and Modifications) Order 2012 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2012/700/article/2) |
| Revenue Scotland | Revenue Scotland and Tax Powers Act 2014 (Consequential Provisions and Modifications) Order 2014 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2014/3294/article/2) |
| Food Standards Scotland | Food (Scotland) Act 2015 (Consequential Provisions) Order 2015 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2015/444/article/2) |
| Scottish Fiscal Commission | Scottish Fiscal Commission Act 2016 (Consequential Provisions and Modifications) Order 2017 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2017/241/article/2) |
| Consumer Scotland | Consumer Scotland Act 2020 (Consequential Provisions and Modifications) Order 2022 | [art 2](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/uksi/2022/34/article/2) |

Each article 2 is in the same form, for example: "Revenue Scotland is part of the Scottish Administration." The retrieved text does not include the Orders' recitals of their enabling powers. These six were found by searching, not by a complete survey, and the Scottish Court Service is named as it appears in the 2009 Order.

#### 2.5 Why a complete list cannot be given here

Three features combine:

- The Act names three offices and then delegates the rest (s.126(8)(b)), and section 104 Orders add whole bodies by a second route.
- The delegated instruments are numerous, spread over the years since 1999, and amend and revoke one another.
- Only some of those Orders were retrieved in this research, and the 1999 Order's Schedule was not.

The honest answer is therefore **structural**: the Scottish Administration comprises the ministerial office-holders, the three offices in section 126(8)(a), the offices specified from time to time by Order in Council under section 126(8)(b), the bodies declared part of it by section 104 Orders, and the staff of all of them. A named list requires a complete survey of both kinds of Order as they now stand, which this research has not done.

Presenting the Orders above as if they were the whole set would be wrong. They are examples of the mechanism, not an enumeration.

---

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Scotland Act 1998 extends to the United Kingdom and was returned in revised form.
- **Limit of this answer.** The list of section 126(8)(b) offices is **incomplete by acknowledgement**, not by oversight. Four section 126(8)(b) Orders and six section 104 Orders were retrieved out of an unknown larger number, and no attempt is made here to state the current consolidated position.
- **Why that is the right answer.** Where the enabling provision delegates open-endedly and the instruments cannot all be retrieved, stating the framework and disclosing the gap is more useful, and more accurate, than a partial list presented as complete.
