### Summary Answer (BLUF)

The duties in the Procurement Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 bite on a **regulated procurement**, and a
procurement is regulated when the contract is a **regulated contract**. Under **section 3(1)** a
contract is a regulated contract if it is a public contract, its estimated value is equal to or
greater than the contract threshold, and it is not an excluded contract.

**Section 3(2)** sets the contract threshold:

| Type of contract | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Public contract (other than a public works contract) | **£50,000** |
| Public works contract | **£2,000,000** |

So goods and services contracts are caught at **£50,000** and works contracts at **£2,000,000**.
These are the 2014 Act's own thresholds. They are not the thresholds of the Public Contracts
(Scotland) Regulations 2015 or of the Procurement Act 2023, which are higher and govern a
separately defined category.

### Detailed Analysis

#### Regulated procurement and regulated contract

Section 2 defines a regulated procurement as any procedure carried out by a contracting authority in
relation to the award of a proposed regulated contract, including seeking offers and selecting
economic operators, and the award of a regulated contract.

Section 3(1) makes a contract a regulated contract where all three of the following hold:

1. it is a public contract;
2. the estimated value of the contract (see section 5) is equal to or greater than the contract
   threshold; and
3. it is not an excluded contract.

All three are required. Meeting the financial threshold alone is not enough.

#### The threshold figures

Section 3(2) states the contract threshold in a table: **£50,000** for a public contract other than
a public works contract, and **£2,000,000** for a public works contract.

#### Estimated value

Section 5(1) provides that the estimated value of a contract is the value of the total consideration,
**not including value added tax**, which the contracting authority expects to be payable under or by
virtue of the contract. Section 5(2) lets the Scottish Ministers make further provision by
regulations about how estimated value is determined.

#### Whether the figures have changed

The Act contains a power to amend the table in section 3(2) by order. Nothing retrieved shows that
power having been exercised to change the £50,000 or £2,000,000 figures, so they stand as enacted.

The nearest instrument, the Public Procurement (Agreement on Government Procurement) (Thresholds)
(Miscellaneous Amendments) (Scotland) Regulations 2023 (SSI 2023/300), amends threshold figures in
the Public Contracts (Scotland) Regulations 2015, the Utilities Contracts (Scotland) Regulations
2016 and the Concession Contracts (Scotland) Regulations 2016, for example substituting
"£5,372,609" for "£5,336,937" in the Concession Contracts Regulations. It does not touch section
3(2) of the 2014 Act.

#### The neighbouring regimes, and why they are not the answer

Section 41 defines a **higher value regulated procurement** as a regulated procurement to which the
Public Contracts Regulations or **section 115A of the Procurement Act 2023** apply. A number of
duties in the 2014 Act are then expressly disapplied for higher value regulated procurements.

Two other figures in the Act are easy to mistake for the threshold and are not it. Section 15
applies where the estimated value is **£4,000,000** or more (community benefit requirements), and an
authority has significant procurement expenditure in a year where the sum of estimated values of its
regulated procurements reaches **£5,000,000** (procurement strategy duties).

### Jurisdiction & Status

Scotland. The Procurement Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 (asp 2014/12) extends to Scotland only and is
in force, status "revised".
