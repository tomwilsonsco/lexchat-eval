### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

The timetable is split across **two** Acts, and this is the point most easily missed.

The Water Industry (Scotland) Act 2002 imposes the duties but **leaves the timing to Ministers' direction**. The fixed deadline comes from the **Public Finance and Accountability (Scotland) Act 2000**, section 21(2): the account "must be sent to the Auditor General **not later than 6 months after the end of the financial year** to which the account relates."

An answer that reads only the 2002 Act will conclude, wrongly, that no statutory deadline exists.

| Duty | Timing | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare account of income and expenditure | for each financial year, per Ministers' directions | 2002 Act, sch A1 para 14(a) |
| Send account to Auditor General | by such time as Ministers direct, and in any event **not later than 6 months** after the year end | 2002 Act, sch A1 para 14(b); **2000 Act, s.21(2)** |
| Report on exercise of functions, submit to Ministers and publish | **as soon as practicable** after the end of each financial year | 2002 Act, s.5(1)(a) |

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The Commission and its accounts duty

The Water Industry Commission for Scotland is established by section 1(1) of the 2002 Act ([asp/2002/3, s.1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2002/3/section/1)), with the general function under section 1(2) of promoting the interests of persons whose premises are connected to the public water supply or sewerage system.

Its accounts duty sits in schedule A1:

> **Paragraph 14** - The Commission must -
> (a) prepare for each financial year, **in accordance with directions given by the Scottish Ministers**, an account of the Commission's income and expenditure, and
> (b) send the account, **by such time as the Scottish Ministers may direct**, to the Auditor General for Scotland for auditing.

([asp/2002/3, sch A1](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2002/3/schedule/A1))

Both limbs are expressed as subject to ministerial direction. Read alone, the 2002 Act supplies **no date**.

#### 2.2 Where the deadline actually is

The Public Finance and Accountability (Scotland) Act 2000 supplies it, and does so generally rather than by naming the Commission:

> **Section 21 - Audit of accounts**
> (1) This section and section 22 apply in relation to any account which is, by virtue of any enactment or prerogative instrument, required (in whatever words) to be -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) audited by the Auditor General, or
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **sent to the Auditor General for auditing**.
> (2) **The account must be sent to the Auditor General not later than 6 months after the end of the financial year to which the account relates.**
> (3) The account is to be audited by - (a) the Auditor General, or (b) a qualified person appearing to the Auditor General to be suitable for appointment ...

([asp/2000/1, s.21](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2000/1/section/21))

The chain is worth stating explicitly, because it is what a reader has to follow to find the deadline:

1. Schedule A1 paragraph 14(b) of the 2002 Act requires the account to be **sent to the Auditor General for auditing**.
2. That engages section 21(1)(b) of the 2000 Act, which applies to **any** account so required "by virtue of any enactment", "in whatever words".
3. Section 21(2) then imposes the 6-month backstop.

A ministerial direction under paragraph 14(b) can require the account sooner, but cannot extend beyond the statutory 6 months.

Section 19 of the 2000 Act ([asp/2000/1, s.19](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2000/1/section/19)) carries the parallel duty to prepare accounts for persons paid out of the Scottish Consolidated Fund, and section 19(4) requires accounts prepared under it to follow directions issued by the Scottish Ministers.

#### 2.3 What the audit must cover

> **Section 22 - Audit of accounts: further provisions**
> (1) The auditor's report on an account in relation to which this section applies ... must set out the auditor's findings on the following matters -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) whether the expenditure and receipts shown in the account were incurred or applied in accordance with - (i) any enactment by virtue of which the expenditure was incurred or the income received, (ii) the Budget Act or Acts for the financial year ... and (iii) sections 4 to 7,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) where sums have been paid out of the Fund for the purpose of meeting such expenditure, whether the sums were [applied accordingly] ...

([asp/2000/1, s.22](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2000/1/section/22))

This is a **regularity** audit obligation, going to whether money was spent as authorised, and it sits alongside the true-and-fair question.

#### 2.4 The annual report on functions, and what it must contain

> **Section 5 - Annual reports by, and information from, the Commission**
> (1) The Commission must -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **as soon as practicable after the end of each financial year**, submit to the Scottish Ministers, **and publish**, a report on the exercise of the Commission's functions during that year, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) provide the Scottish Ministers with such information regarding the exercise of those functions as they may require.
> (2) A report under subsection (1)(a) **must include a summary of** -
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) action taken by the Commission during the year ... in response to - (i) any advice, information or representation made to it by Citizens Advice Scotland under section 8(1)(a) or (c) of the Consumers, Estate Agents and Redress Act 2007, and (ii) any proposals made to it under section 8(1)(b) of that Act, and
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) where the Commission has decided during that year not to take any action in response to any such advice, information, proposal or representation ...

([asp/2002/3, s.5](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2002/3/section/5))

Two features to note. The obligation is to **submit and publish**, so publication is a statutory duty rather than a matter of practice. And subsection (2) is the only express **content** requirement placed on the report: the Citizens Advice Scotland summary. The general subject matter, "the exercise of the Commission's functions during that year", is otherwise left at large.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** Both the Water Industry (Scotland) Act 2002 and the Public Finance and Accountability (Scotland) Act 2000 extend to Scotland, and were returned in revised form.
- **Which body.** The Commission's constitutive provisions have been reworked over time, and the Water Services etc. (Scotland) Act 2005 also contains a schedule 1 dealing with the Commission in materially similar terms. This answer cites schedule A1 to the 2002 Act as the operative source.
- **Out of scope.** The Public Services Reform (Scotland) Act 2010 is general public-body reform legislation and is **not** a source of the Commission's reporting timetable. Citing it in answer to this question is a scope error rather than a small inaccuracy.
- **Not addressed.** Laying before the Scottish Parliament is not among the duties retrieved for the Commission. Section 5(1)(a) requires submission to Ministers and publication, not laying, and no laying requirement was found for the Commission's own report or account.
