# Power of the Scottish Ministers to establish a national bus fare cap scheme

## 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

Two different powers have to be told apart, and the question turns on the word **national**.

**An express maximum-fares power does exist**, at section 3C(3)(b) of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001 ([asp/2001/2, s.3C](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/3C)): a bus services improvement partnership scheme may impose requirements about "the maximum fares that may be charged". But it belongs to a **local transport authority** for its own area, not to the Scottish Ministers, so it cannot produce a national scheme.

**For a national scheme the Scottish Ministers must use their grant powers.** The provision to cite is **section 38 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001** ([asp/2001/2, s.38](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/38)): Ministers may make grants to operators of eligible bus services, "subject to such conditions ... as the Scottish Ministers may determine". A cap on the fare charged is such a condition. **Section 70 of the same Act** ([asp/2001/2, s.70](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/70)) is a broader alternative: a general power to make grants or loans to any persons for any purposes relating to transport.

The concessionary travel powers are **not** the answer. A "travel concession" is statutorily defined as a reduction or waiver of the fare **for eligible persons**, which is a different instrument from a cap applying to passengers generally.

## 2. Detailed Analysis

### 2.1 Section 38 of the 2001 Act, the operator grant power

> **Section 38 — Grants to bus service operators**
>
> (1) The Scottish Ministers may make grants to operators of eligible bus services towards their costs in operating those services.
> (2) The Scottish Ministers may make provision by regulations as to the method of calculation of grants.
> (3) Subject to any regulations under subsection (2) above, grants under this section shall be of such amount and **subject to such conditions** (including conditions requiring their repayment in specified circumstances) **as the Scottish Ministers may determine**.
> (4) A determination under subsection (3) above may be made either generally or in relation to particular cases or classes of case.
> (5) In this section "eligible bus services" means services of a class using public service vehicles (or such services using a class of public service vehicles) prescribed by regulations made by the Scottish Ministers.

([asp/2001/2, s.38](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/38))

Three features make this the natural vehicle for a fare cap:

- **Conditions are at large.** Subsection (3) does not enumerate permitted conditions. A requirement that a participating operator charge no more than a stated single fare is within the ordinary meaning of a condition of grant.
- **It can be applied generally.** Subsection (4) allows a determination to be made "either generally or in relation to particular cases or classes of case", which is what makes a scheme national rather than operator-by-operator.
- **Repayment is available as enforcement.** Subsection (3) expressly contemplates conditions requiring repayment, so a cap can be enforced by clawback without any further statutory machinery.

The limit is subsection (5): the power bites on "eligible bus services", a class prescribed by regulations. The scope of any cap is therefore bounded by those regulations.

### 2.2 Section 70 of the 2001 Act, the general transport grant power

> **Section 70 — Grants for transport-related purposes**
>
> (1) The Scottish Ministers may make grants or loans to any persons for any purposes relating to transport.

([asp/2001/2, s.70](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/70))

This is wider than section 38 in two respects: the recipient is "any persons", not only operators of eligible bus services, and the purpose is "any purposes relating to transport", not only operating costs. Where a scheme's design falls outside the "eligible bus services" boundary in section 38(5), section 70 is the fallback. The remainder of the section (subsections (1A) to (1B)) concerns grants for acquiring land blighted by transport orders and is not relevant here.

### 2.3 The express maximum-fares power, and why it is not national

Section 3C of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001 governs the service standards a bus services improvement partnership scheme may impose:

> (3) An operational service standard may, in particular, impose requirements about —
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) the vehicles which are used to provide services,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **the maximum fares that may be charged** for particular journeys, or for journeys of particular descriptions on services to which the scheme applies,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) ticketing and the manner in which entitlement to travel may be evidenced,
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) the pricing of multi-operator travel cards, ...

([asp/2001/2, s.3C](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/3C))

This is a direct power to cap fares, and it is worth citing for that reason. Two limits stop it answering the question as asked:

- **The holder is a local transport authority, not the Scottish Ministers.** A partnership scheme is made under section 3B alongside a partnership plan, and section 3A(1) provides that "**a local transport authority** may, if they consider it appropriate to do so, make a bus services improvement partnership plan ... in relation to the whole or part of their area" ([asp/2001/2, s.3A](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/3A)).
- **The reach is the scheme's area.** Section 3C(5) provides that a service standard "has effect only in relation to so much of a local service as is provided in" the scheme area.

A national cap would therefore require every local transport authority to make a scheme in identical terms, rather than a single act of the Scottish Ministers. That is why the grant route in 2.1 and 2.2 is the answer to a question about a **national** scheme.

### 2.4 What the concessionary travel powers do instead

Two powers create **travel concession schemes**, and neither creates a fare cap.

**Section 40 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2005** ([asp/2005/12, s.40](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2005/12/section/40)) lets the Scottish Ministers "by order, make national travel concession schemes", operating throughout Scotland or in part of it. The defined terms are decisive:

> (7) In this section —
> "**eligible person**" in relation to a national travel concession scheme, means a person who is of such description as the Scottish Ministers may by order specify;
> "**travel concession**", in relation to a journey, means — reduction of the fare ... for the journey below the amount applicable to an adult who is not entitled to any reduction; or waiver of such fare.

A concession is thus defined **by reference to an adult who is not entitled to any reduction**, whose fare the scheme leaves untouched. The instrument reduces or waives the fare for a specified class of person. It does not set a ceiling on what everyone else pays.

**Section 68 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001** ([asp/2001/2, s.68](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2001/2/section/68)) is the local counterpart: the Scottish Ministers may by order require a specified local transport authority, or two or more acting jointly, to make a travel concession scheme for a specified area. It is a power to direct authorities, over an area that is "the whole or any part of the area, or combined area" of those authorities, so it is not a route to a national scheme in its own right.

### 2.5 A worked example of the concessionary route

The National Bus Travel Concession Scheme for Young Persons (Scotland) Order 2021 ([ssi/2021/175](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ssi/2021/175)) shows the concessionary machinery in operation. Article 3 provides that "This Order establishes a national travel concession scheme ... to be known as the National Bus Travel Concession Scheme for Young Persons", and article 2 defines "eligible journey" as having "the same meaning as in section 40(7) of the 2005 Act", confirming the Order operates under section 40 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2005.

Article 5 sets the rate: "The rate of travel concession in the scheme is to be a waiver of the fare". A total waiver for a specified group, not a cap for everyone, which is exactly the distinction in 2.4.

### 2.6 The parallel with England

Section 38 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001 is drawn in near-identical terms to **section 154 of the Transport Act 2000** ([ukpga/2000/38, s.154](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/ukpga/2000/38/section/154)), the England and Wales operator grant power, down to both provisions repealing section 92 of the Finance Act 1965 and section 111 of the Transport Act 1985. The two jurisdictions therefore reach a fare cap by the same statutory route, a conditional operator grant, under separate Acts.

## 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** The Transport (Scotland) Act 2001 (asp 2) and the Transport (Scotland) Act 2005 (asp 12) both extend to Scotland only. The retrieval returned both in revised (as amended) form.
- **Status of the provisions cited.** Sections 38, 68 and 70 of the 2001 Act and section 40 of the 2005 Act were all returned as current revised text, with no indication of repeal.
- **Limit of this answer.** That the Scottish Ministers hold no power to fix bus fares nationally by order is a conclusion from the searches recorded in `searches.json`, which covered the Transport (Scotland) Act 2001, the Transport (Scotland) Act 2005 and Scottish concessionary travel instruments. It is a negative established by not finding such a power in that material, which is weaker evidence than a positive citation, and it is the point most worth a reviewer's attention. The express but local power at section 3C(3)(b) is by contrast positively established.
- **Out of scope.** Whether any fare cap has actually been established in Scotland, and on what terms, is a question of policy and administrative action rather than legislation, and the legislation database cannot answer it.
