### 1. Summary Answer (BLUF)

**Partly.** The Care Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 is **not** wholly in force, and it is **not** wholly uncommenced.

The Act received Royal Assent on **22 July 2025**. Section 39 brought **section 30 and the final Part into force on 23 July 2025**, the day after. Everything else comes into force only when the Scottish Ministers appoint a day by regulations.

The failure this question exists to catch is stopping there. Reading section 39 tells you the substantive provisions await commencement regulations. It does **not** tell you whether those regulations have since been made. **They have**: the Care Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (Commencement No. 1) Regulations 2025 exist. An answer that reads section 39 and then volunteers that no commencement regulations have been made states a false negative, and does so on a question where the user is relying on the answer to know whether a duty currently binds them.

### 2. Detailed Analysis

#### 2.1 The commencement provision

> **Section 39 - Commencement**
> (1) The following come into force on the day after Royal Assent: **section 30 and this Part**.
> (2) The other provisions of this Act come into force on such day as **the Scottish Ministers may by regulations appoint**.

([asp/2025/9, s.39](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2025/9/section/39))

The Act's own metadata records an enactment date of **22 July 2025**, so subsection (1) operated on **23 July 2025**. The Act has 56 provisions and is held in revised form with Scotland extent.

Section 39 splits the Act into two populations:

| Population | Status | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Section 30, and the Part containing section 39 (the final, general Part: [s.37 Ancillary provision](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2025/9/section/37), [s.38 Regulation-making powers](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2025/9/section/38), s.39 itself and [s.40 Short title](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/id/asp/2025/9/section/40)) | **In force 23 July 2025** | s.39(1) |
| All other provisions, including the substantive care reform duties | In force **only when appointed** by regulations | s.39(2) |

Note the shape of subsection (1). It commences the machinery, the ancillary provision power and the short title, plus one substantive section. This is the ordinary pattern for a Scottish Act, and it is exactly the pattern that misleads: an Act can be "commenced" in the sense that some of it is live while none of the provisions a user cares about are.

#### 2.2 The step that must not be skipped

Section 39(2) is a **pointer to a further instrument**, not an answer. Answering "is this Act in force?" requires a second step: find out which, if any, commencement regulations have been made under section 39(2), and what they brought into force and when.

The Care Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (Commencement No. 1) Regulations 2025 have been made. They could **not** be found by any of the four searches run here, by full title, by short title, or by subject. That is a retrieval limitation, and it is the trap: **failing to find a commencement instrument is not evidence that none exists.**

The retrieval makes that distinction concrete. The same searches readily returned commencement instruments for two other Acts of the same session:

- The Scottish Elections (Representation and Reform) Act 2025 (Commencement No. 1) Regulations 2025 (`ssi/2025/106`)
- The Victims, Witnesses, and Justice Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (Commencement No. 1 and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2025 (`ssi/2025/393`)

So the search tool can and does surface commencement SSIs when it reaches them. It simply did not reach this one. A correct answer reports what section 39 provides, reports that it could not retrieve the commencement regulations, and stops short of asserting that none were made.

#### 2.3 How the answer should be framed

- **Correct.** "Partly in force. Section 30 and the final Part came into force on 23 July 2025; the remaining provisions are commenced by regulations under section 39(2). Commencement No. 1 Regulations have been made, so you should check them for the specific provision you are asking about."
- **Acceptable.** The same, with "I could not retrieve the commencement regulations, so please check them directly" in place of any claim about their contents.
- **Wrong.** "The substantive provisions are not in force and no commencement regulations have been identified or made."

The difference between the second and the third is one clause, and it is the whole question.

### 3. Jurisdiction & Status

- **Extent.** Scotland. `asp/2025/9`, held in revised form, 56 provisions, extent Scotland.
- **Royal Assent.** 22 July 2025, so section 39(1) took effect 23 July 2025.
- **Currency.** Commencement is a moving target. Any answer to this question is accurate only as at the date it is given, and a further Commencement No. 2 or later instrument may since have been made. A correct answer says which day it speaks as at.
- **Not addressed.** Which specific substantive provisions the Commencement No. 1 Regulations brought into force, and on what dates, are not stated here: the instrument could not be retrieved, so its contents are not asserted.
